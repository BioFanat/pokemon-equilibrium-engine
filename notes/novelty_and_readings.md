# Novelty check (N1-N5) and AGT reading list

Date: 2026-10-11. Tags: [verified] = metadata checked in Undermind and/or content checked in the PDF/source by me; [snippet-only] = metadata from Undermind/web snippet or abstract only, content not read.

## Part A. Novelty verdicts

Summary: N1 and N2 are open for Pokemon (no Pokemon bot does them). N3 is partially done (Jaxcalibur heuristic only) in Pokemon and is a standard idea in the poker/FoW-chess lineage, so the novelty claim must be "first for Pokemon", not "first". N4 and N5 are open in Pokemon. Biggest framing risk: N2 and N3 are essentially ReBeL / DeepStack / Obscuro machinery; cite them and claim the adaptation, not the idea.

### Chronoquazicalar (repo TurboRx/Chronoquazicalar, cloned at commit 6e9a7c6, 2026-10-08) [verified, source read]

Root "CFR" setup (answers N1):
- `search/regret_matching.py:9-62` `solve_matrix_game_cfr(payoff_matrix, ...)` is plain two-player zero-sum regret matching on ONE (m x n) matrix, with average strategies returned. No types, no belief weights. Docstring: "Computes approximate Nash equilibrium mixed strategies for zero-sum matrix games."
- `search/puct_search.py:149-155` (inside `search()`): `payoff_submatrix = root.q_matrix[np.ix_(valid_acts_p1, valid_acts_p2)]` then `get_mixed_action(...)`. The matrix is the Q-matrix estimated by pUCT in a single concrete world.
- `search/puct_search.py:169-254` `search_belief_worlds()`: loops over sampled worlds, calls `self.search(world_state, ...)` per world (222-228), then `aggregated_strategy += w_strat` (230), normalizes (236-240), and `chosen_act = int(np.argmax(final_strategy))` (242). Docstring (177-179): "Averages Monte Carlo tree strategies and action-values across worlds."
- Verdict on N1: this is per-world solve then average (strategy fusion); the opponent's strategy is NOT conditioned on its type. Not agent-form. N1 is open.
- Other observations: inside the tree, action selection is a pUCT with row/column mean of Q against a uniform opponent (`puct_search.py:306-324`), not regret matching, so the README's "simultaneous-move CFR" is only a root post-processing of the Q matrix. Early-game bypass (`puct_search.py:108-123`, and called per world at 222) returns argmax of the network policy when <=2 opponent mons are revealed, i.e. no search at all early. Bot uses 4-6 worlds (`bot_client.py:~623-640`: `num_worlds = 4` or `6`).
- Information-horizon truncation, `puct_search.py:263-265`: "truncate search if simulated actions fall outside plausible prior horizon (< 2% prior probability)" - a prior-probability cutoff for depth>0, similar in spirit to Jaxcalibur's "surprising" truncation, not a leakage model.

"Bayesian set inference" (answers N2):
- `engine/randbats_knowledge.py:89-114` `get_candidate_sets()`: keeps sets whose `movepool` is a superset of the revealed moves (`norm_revealed.issubset(set_moves)`), falls back to all sets if none match. Hard-evidence consistency filter only. README (line ~59) says "Dynamically filters candidate roles, movepools, abilities, and Tera types as opponent moves are revealed."
- `engine/belief_sampler.py:70-98` `_sample_moves_for_species`: `chosen_set = r.choice(candidate_sets)` - UNIFORM over consistent sets (no set-count weights, unlike Foul Play), then fills unrevealed moves by shuffling the movepool.
- `engine/belief_sampler.py:177-190`: unrevealed bench species drawn by `r.sample(available_species, ...)` uniformly from all species (no team-generation constraints, no use of what the opponent chose to switch to/reveal). 
- No likelihood term, no use of what the opponent did as a function of its hidden set. Verdict on N2: not done (not even set-count weighting). N2 is open. The words "Bayesian set inference" in the README overstate the code: it is a consistency filter.

Leakage (answers N3): grep for conceal/leak/exploit/EXP3/opponent-model over all .py files found nothing. The belief sampler only models the opponent's uncertainty about nothing; our team is treated as known to both (P1 side built from `battle.team`, `belief_sampler.py:112-145`). Verdict: not done.

### Foul Play (pmariglia/foul-play, cloned commit 8f541f8) [verified, source read]
- `fp/search/random_battles.py:19-65` `prepare_random_battles`: samples sets with `random.choices(..., weights=[s.pkmn_set.count ...])` among remaining sets; unrevealed mons via `sample_randombattle_pokemon` with Showdown team-generation constraints (`random_battles.py:~75-130`).
- `fp/battle/inference.py` (`check_speed_ranges`, `check_choicescarf`, `get_damage_dealt`, `update_dataset_possibilities`, `check_heavydutyboots`, `check_opponent_hiddenpower`): all inference from hard evidence (speed ordering, damage rolls, item effects), e.g. line ~403 log "could not have gone first - setting it's item to choicescarf". No likelihood of the opponent's CHOICE given its set.
- grep for bayes/posterior/likelihood/conceal/exp3/exploit found no such mechanism. Verdicts: N2 not done (hard-evidence only, the strongest of the open-source bots in this respect); N3, N4, N5 not done.

### Jaxcalibur write-up (jaxcalibur.github.io, fetched via WebFetch; summary only, page is the only source) [snippet-only]
- Sets: "sampling possible 'worlds' of the opponent's unknown Pokemon/moves/items/etc. using its network's predictions"; "maintains the item and ability predictions across turns so it can remember revealed information." The page does not say beliefs are updated on opponent choices beyond revealed facts (the network may implicitly do this from history; unknown).
- N3 (partial, heuristic): "Search for the opponent is truncated whenever Jaxcalibur does something 'surprising'" with example "using a move predicted with <65% likelihood from the opponent's perspective"; the stated purpose is that this "prevents search from leaking hidden information such as Tera types to the simulated opponent." This is the leakage idea, implemented as a heuristic cutoff with no explicit opponent belief about our sets and no regularization.
- Search: pUCT variant sampling edges "according to a mixture of the positive regret and network prior"; depth 4, 20,480 rollouts over 32 worlds. Page has nothing on cross-battle exploitation (N4) or probing/value of information (N5).

### Other work
- PokaiTrainer (Yu, arXiv 2608.29197, v1 2026-08-29, v2 2026-09-28) [snippet-only, abstract page only]: "Bayesian matrix game over public belief states", Student-of-Games-style GT-CFR, VGC open team sheets. Abstract page does not state the belief update rule or any leakage handling; read the HTML full text (arxiv.org/html/2608.29197v2) before claiming N2/N3 are absent there. Hidden info is stated as opponent's "reserves and stat allocations".
- Metamon (Grigsby et al., arXiv 2504.04395) and PokeAgent Challenge (arXiv 2603.15563) [snippet-only]: offline RL / benchmark; opponent's choices are part of the environment, no explicit belief algebra.
- Poker/chess lineage for N2/N3 (the intellectual precedents; N2 and N3 are NOT new in general):
  - ReBeL (Brown et al. 2020, arXiv 2007.13544) [verified in PDF]: PBS = public state plus both players' range; "players can update their belief distribution about which card they are holding via Bayes' Rule" after each action sampled from the announced policy, "policies are common knowledge". That IS N2 (belief update using the equilibrium policy). Also tracks both players' beliefs (the N3 object).
  - DeepStack / safe-and-nested subgame solving [metadata verified, content snippet-only]: re-solving keeps our range and constrains the opponent's counterfactual values so the solve cannot let the opponent do better than in the blueprint. This is the Aumann-Maschler / concealment idea in practical form (N3).
  - Zhang & Sandholm, "Subgame solving without common knowledge" (arXiv 2106.06068) and Obscuro for Fog-of-War chess (arXiv 2506.01242) [abstract verified]: search with low-order knowledge because common-knowledge closure is too large; abstract of the latter says such games "require reasoning about information gathering, the opponent's knowledge, signaling". Directly relevant to N3 and N5 in a simultaneous/partial-observation setting, and the most credible precedent for doing N3 approximately in a large game.
  - Goodman, Re-determinizing IS-MCTS in Hanabi (arXiv 1902.06075) [abstract verified]: names and fixes "leakage of hidden information into opponent models" in determinized search; a direct precedent for Jaxcalibur-style truncation as a leakage fix.
  - DeepNash (Perolat et al., Science 2022) [snippet-only]: model-free regularized Nash dynamics, no search, so no explicit belief.
  - Ganzfried & Sun, Bayesian Opponent Exploitation (arXiv 1603.03491, CIG 2018) [abstract verified], Southey et al. "Bayes' Bluff" (arXiv 1207.1411) [snippet-only], Li et al. "Opponent-model search" (AAAI 2024) [abstract verified]: N4-side, cross-game opponent modelling.
  - Radboud BSc thesis (van der Heijden) sketch, per your brief, unimplemented.
- No paper found (several searches, Undermind plus web) that implements type-conditional / agent-form Bayesian solving with equilibrium-consistent belief updates in a Pokemon bot. Absence evidence only; searched the Undermind global corpus and web search, not exhaustive.

### Math cautions to give students
1. N1 equality: v(b) = max_x sum_theta b(theta) min_beta (M_theta^T x)_beta is correct as the Bayes-Nash value when the opponent type is hidden from US and the opponent knows its own type but not our hidden set, AND types are independent across players (Hartline et al. restrict to product type distributions, see their text "conditional independence property is essential"). In Randbats the two teams are generated independently, so this holds at the root; it breaks after beliefs are updated on joint history only if our own hidden set also matters to the opponent (two-sided case), in which case the right object is a PBS, not a one-sided Bayesian matrix game.
2. v(b) is concave in b only for the max-min form with a FIXED x-space; it is the value function of a game with one-sided incomplete information, and the Aumann-Maschler cav(u) result is the repeated-game analog. Do not claim more than this without a proof.
3. "Information has nonnegative value in zero-sum games" is true for a player who can ignore the extra signal while the opponent's strategy space is unchanged (Blackwell-style, one player). Gossner-Mertens is about the two-sided comparison (more info to one player and the effect on the value); I could only read the introduction (it states the Hirshleifer paradox for non-zero-sum games and says "For one agent, the value of information is known to be positive"), not the theorem. Mark the citation as snippet-only until the PDF is read.

## Part B. Reading list (14 items + textbook)

Venue/year/identifier given; tag in brackets.

1. [verified] Hartline, Syrgkanis, Tardos. "No-Regret Learning in Bayesian Games." NeurIPS 2015, pp. 3061-3069. arXiv 1507.00418. Why: Bayesian game as agent normal form (each type a player; read in PDF, "also called the agent normal form representation"), and no-regret dynamics converge to Bayesian coarse correlated equilibrium. Supports per-type regret matchers (N1). Note it requires independent types across players. Abstract and agent-form text checked in PDF; I did not check the convergence proof.
2. [verified metadata] Freund, Schapire. "Adaptive game playing using multiplicative weights." Games and Economic Behavior 29(1-2), 1999, 79-103. doi:10.1006/game.1999.0738. Why: MW dynamics in zero-sum games converge to the minimax value (course unit on dynamics).
3. [verified metadata] Hart, Mas-Colell. "A simple adaptive procedure leading to correlated equilibrium." Econometrica 68(5), 2000, 1127-1150. doi:10.1111/1468-0262.00153. Why: regret matching, the algorithm both Chronoquazicalar and Foul Play-style code uses.
4. [verified metadata] Koller, Megiddo, von Stengel. "Efficient computation of equilibria for extensive two-person games." Games and Economic Behavior 14(2), 1996, 247-259. doi:10.1006/game.1996.0051. Why: sequence-form LP; exact solution of the (type-conditional) game, polynomial size, as an alternative to CFR. (The 1994 STOC paper "Fast algorithms for finding randomized strategies in game trees" is the conference precursor [snippet-only, not looked up].)
5. [verified metadata] Zinkevich, Johanson, Bowling, Piccione. "Regret minimization in games with incomplete information." NeurIPS 2007, pp. 1729-1736. doi:10.7939/R3Q23R282. Why: CFR; per-infoset regret decomposition, the template for N1 at scale.
6. [verified metadata; ReBeL text checked] Brown, Sandholm. "Safe and nested subgame solving for imperfect-information games." NeurIPS 2017, arXiv 1705.02955; Moravcik et al. "DeepStack: Expert-level artificial intelligence in heads-up no-limit poker." Science 356, 2017, 508-513, doi:10.1126/science.aam6960; Brown, Bakhtin, Lerer, Gong. "Combining deep reinforcement learning and search for imperfect-information games" (ReBeL). NeurIPS 2020, arXiv 2007.13544; Schmid et al. "Student of Games." Science Advances 9, 2023, doi:10.1126/sciadv.adg3256 (Undermind lists year 2021, the arXiv 2112.03178 preprint). Why: PBS, range tracking, safe resolve: the machinery for N2 and N3. Treat as one reading block (4 papers); ReBeL's Bayes-rule belief update checked in PDF.
7. [snippet-only for the classic; metadata verified for the follow-up] Aumann, Maschler. "Repeated Games with Incomplete Information." MIT Press, 1995 (collects 1960s-70s Mathematica reports with Stearns). Why: cav(u) and the non-revealing game, the theory behind N3. Undermind lookup gave only secondary papers (Gensbittel Math. Oper. Res. 2015, de Meyer-Rosenberg 1999); the book itself was not looked up or read. Cite as a book and verify the year/publisher before finalizing the bibliography.
8. [verified metadata] Kamenica, Gentzkow. "Bayesian persuasion." American Economic Review 101(6), 2011, 2590-2615. doi:10.1257/aer.101.6.2590. Why: concavification of the sender's value over posteriors; the same math as cav(u), clean 1-lecture treatment of concealment vs revelation. Undermind's record shows a 2024 book-series entry; the DOI is the AER paper.
9. [verified metadata] Bergemann, Morris. "Bayes correlated equilibrium and the comparison of information structures in games." Theoretical Economics 11(2), 2016, 487-522. doi:10.3982/TE1808. Why: Bayes CE as the set of outcomes over all information structures; relates to the agent-form CCE of item 1 and to value-of-information comparisons.
