## 1. Summary

1. **Closest prior work is PokaiTrainer (Yu 2026, arXiv 2608.29197)**. It already does what our agent-form LP does: each decision is a "Bayesian matrix game over public belief states", with the agent using one strategy across worlds and the opponent a per-world strategy. It solves with alternating-update linear CFR (300 root / 100 interior iterations). It reports no PIMC/determinization comparison, no value-of-concealment measurement, and no RM+ vs OMWU comparison. Cite it as concurrent related work. Our novelty is not the formulation but the PIMC-vs-agent-form comparison, the blend-loss bound and its empirical check, and the solver and exploitation findings.
2. **PIMC theory is qualitative.** Long et al. 2010 define strategy fusion and non-locality and study synthetic-tree properties (leaf correlation, bias, disambiguation factor), and state explicitly that they give no worst-case loss bound. Arjonilla et al. 2024 (EPIMC) prove only monotonicity of a count of fusion-creating infostates in lookahead depth (Props. 1-3), not an error bound. I found no PIMC error bound of the Hausdorff/Lipschitz type. I cannot assert it is novel, because my search was limited (Whitehouse's thesis, Frank & Basin full text, Furtak & Buro were not read). The closest quantitative analogue is Li et al. 2024, with L1 distance and diameter bounds on risk and opportunity under wrong type beliefs.
3. **The 4·delta_H bound looks like a standard Lipschitz fact** (my inference): v is 1-Lipschitz in the Hausdorff distance of the column sets (min over a set of linear functionals is 1-Lipschitz in Hausdorff distance, for x in the simplex and sup-norm payoffs). The factor 4 would come from two-sided blending. This should be stated as a short lemma, not as a headline result. The empirical validation on 20k real decisions (max ratio 0.086) is the new part.
4. **Solver observation (RM+ at 100 iterations ~ exact LP; OMWU at 1000 much worse)** is consistent with the literature. OMWU's last-iterate convergence constants can be arbitrarily bad (Cai et al. 2024), and RM+ is parameter-free and works well in practice although its theory is weak: last-iterate convergence is proven only under strict NE (Cai et al. 2023), and it can be unstable (Farina et al. 2023). Hyperparameter (step size) sensitivity and averaging vs last iterate are the likely confounds. Check which iterate you played.
5. **Exploitation findings.** The PokaiTrainer result that a local best response (LBR) beats the argmax of its CFR solution 77.7% vs 49.6% against the mixed strategy is consistent with ours: argmax and pure maximin beat sampling only because MCTS is non-adaptive. Against an adaptive opponent, argmax is exploitable. A positive-sum story needs a safety guarantee (see section 5).
6. **Value of information zero at depth 1.** I located no paper stating this directly. Belief-dependent leaf values are standard in DeepStack, ReBeL, Student of Games and PokaiTrainer (value network returns one value per world).

## 2. Per-topic notes

### 2.1 PIMC, strategy fusion, non-locality
- Lon10 (Long, Sturtevant, Buro, Furtak, AAAI 2010) [PDF-read], p.2: "Strategy fusion arises because PIMC search (incorrectly) believes it can use a different strategy in each world, whereas in reality there are situations (or information sets) which consist of multiple perfect information scenarios." and "Non-locality ... a node's value may depend on other regions of the game tree not contained within its subtree, primarily due to the opponent's ability to direct the play towards regions of the tree that he knows ... are favorable for him." Both errors persist "irrespective of the number of hypothetical worlds examined." Synthetic trees parametrised by leaf correlation lc, bias b, disambiguation factor df. **No quantitative bound**; only empirical (e.g. 0.42 TP/deal mean loss on 3,000 unresolved Skat deals vs CFR). Paper notes PIMC could be much worse against an exploiting opponent (Conclusion, p.7).
- Fra98 (Frank & Basin 1998, Artif. Intell.) [unverified, no PDF]: origin of the strategy-fusion analysis in bridge. Cite, not read.
- Arj24 (Arjonilla, Saffidine, Cazenave, CoG 2024, EPIMC) [PDF-read]: Def. 1 "creates strategy fusion if there are h, h' in H(s) such that pi(h) != pi(h')"; Props. 1-3: increasing depth d never increases the strategy-fusion count SF, a larger depth strictly reduces it if SF>0, and in finite games some depth eliminates it. The paper "does not give a bound on PIMC's error". Relevant to us: your depth-2 search vs depth-1 is the EPIMC axis.
- Cowling/Powley/Whitehouse ISMCTS: Whi11 (2011 CIG, Dou Di Zhu determinization vs ISMCTS) [unverified, no PDF], Pow14 (Artif. Intell., information capture and reuse) [unverified], Cow12 (ensemble determinization, Magic) [unverified]. Ihara et al. 2018 (Iha18) is ISMCTS in Pokemon: "ISMCTS outperforms the conventional MCTS that uses determinization" [abstract].
- Fur13 Recursive Monte Carlo search (Furtak & Buro 2013) [unverified, no PDF]. Goodman 2019 (re-determinizing ISMCTS in Hanabi) [abstract-level only, not read].
- Web search (EPIMC arXiv 2408.02380 snippets): "did not turn up an explicit error bound for Monte Carlo determinization."

### 2.3 Search plus learning under imperfect information
- DeepStack Mor17 (Science 2017) [located, not read]. Libratus: Bro17 safe and nested subgame solving [PDF-read]: "Resolving guarantees that P2's exploitability will be no higher than the blueprint's (and may be better)" (Sec. 4.2); Theorem 1 is for Reach-Maxmargin with gift lower bounds. The opponent's alternatives are modelled as opt-out payoffs at subgame entry. Pluribus Bro19. Depth-limited solving Bro18 (NeurIPS 2018). ReBeL Bro20 (arXiv 2007.13544), public belief states with RL+search. Student of Games Sch21 (Sci. Adv. 2023). Kov19b value functions for depth-limited solving. Hu21 learned belief search. Kub23 [abstract]: look-ahead search on top of policy networks (trained with R-NaD), critic estimating values for depth-limited search.
- Relevance: all of these use belief-dependent leaf values (vectors over private info), which is exactly what "value of information is zero at depth 1 with scalar state value" says is needed.
- PokaiTrainer Yu26d [PDF-read]: Sec 4.1 "A public belief state beta=(s,b) pairs s with the solver's posterior b in Delta(W(s))"; Sec 4.2 "We solve every decision the agent faces, and every decision node inside a subgame, as a Bayesian matrix game"; leaf V(beta_leaf)=<b, v_theta(beta_leaf)> (Eq. 1); "alternating-update linear CFR (Brown & Sandholm, 2019b) with one regret table for seat 1 and one per world for seat 2"; 300/100/160 iterations (App. E.1). Sec 4.1 concedes seat-1's hidden spreads to the opponent ("conservative"). Reports no PIMC comparison and no concealment-value measurement. Results: 89-61 sets (59.3%, Wilson 51-67%) on ladder (Sec 5.4); PPO self-play (VGC-Bench recipe) beaten 73-27 (App. E.3); "The network's policy alone loses even to a shallow heuristic search" (abstract) -- consistent with our "PPO plateaus far below search." Note Undermind lists a duplicate record Yu26e for the same arXiv id.
- VGC-Bench Ang25 (Angliss et al., AAMAS 2026) [abstract-level], PokeChamp Kar25b (minimax LLM agent, 76%/84% win rates vs LLM/rule bots, abstract).

### 2.4 No-regret dynamics
- CFR+ Tam14; discounted/linear CFR Bro18b (arXiv 1809.04040). Optimistic regret minimization for EFGs Far19. Last-iterate convergence in EFGs Lee21.
- Das18 (Daskalakis & Panageas, ITCS 2019) [abstract-level]: last-iterate convergence of OMWU in zero-sum games (uniqueness assumption).
- Cai24 (Cai et al. 2024) [abstract]: "OMWU ... the best existing last-iterate convergence for OMWU depends on some game-dependent constant that could be arbitrarily large ... for any arbitrarily small delta>0, there exists a 2x2 matrix game such that the algorithm admits a constant duality gap even after 1/delta rounds." This is the cleanest explanation of OMWU-at-1000-iterations being inaccurate.
- Cai23 (last-iterate of regret-matching algorithms) [PDF-read]: Thm 1, p.5: "If a matrix game has a strict Nash equilibrium (x*,y*), RM+ converges in last-iterate"; strict NE "cannot be weakened to ... a unique, non-strict NE, as our empirical counterexample shows"; on a 3x3 instance "for RM+, alternating RM+, and PRM+, the duality gap remains on the order of 10^-1 even after 10^5 iterations". ExRM+ and SPRM+ converge unconditionally (Thms 2, 5). Does NOT compare against OMWU empirically.
- Far23 (Farina et al. 2023) [abstract]: RM+ and predictive RM+ "can be unstable"; fixes give O(T^{1/4}) individual regret.
- Sul26 (Sullins & Kash, arXiv 2610.19016) [abstract]: IR-PRM+ with previous-utility prediction, constant regret, O(1/T) average gap, O(T^{-1/2}) best-iterate gap. Zha25c scale-invariant RM+ [title/abstract-level]. Ana25 convergence of RM in potential games [title].
- MMD Sok22 (Sokota et al.) [PDF-read]: "While CFR+ yields the strongest performance ... both variants of MMD exhibit last-iterate convergent behavior, they also perform competitively with (or better than) CFR" (Sec 4, Fig 2). Update (Eq. 10): pi_{t+1} = argmax E q_t - alpha KL(pi, rho) - (1/eta) KL(pi, pi_t), rho = "magnet". Cen21 (policy extragradient, entropy-regularised games), Mun20 (Fast computation of Nash equilibria in IIGs, R-NaD precursor) [abstract not read].
- R-NaD / DeepNash Per22 (Science 2022) [abstract]: model-free, game-theoretic RL, no search, top-3 on Gravon.
- Comparison with our observation: RM+ needs no step size and no payoff-scale tuning; OMWU's rate depends on step size and game constants. We did not find a paper directly comparing RM+ vs OMWU on small matrix games at 100 vs 1000 iterations; our result is empirical and consistent with theory. Check that the OMWU step size was tuned per payoff scale (Zha25c is on this point).

### 2.5 Exploitation vs safety, opponent models
- Gan15 (Ganzfried & Sandholm, ACM TEAC 2015, safe opponent exploitation) [unverified, no PDF]; Gan16 (Bayesian opponent exploitation in IIGs, arXiv 1603.03491) [PDF-read]: Theorem 2.1 "payoff against the mean of a strategy distribution equals the payoff against the full distribution", so best-responding to the posterior mean = Bayesian best response. Quote: "for all games one can potentially do better by exploiting perceived weaknesses of the opponent than by following a static equilibrium strategy throughout the match." Reports in Table 3 average payoff ~0 for EBBR vs -0.375 for Nash vs ~0.498 for full BR. A full BR to an inaccurate model "can have high exploitability" (Sec 3).
- Joh09 (Johanson & Bowling, data-biased response, AISTATS 2009) and restricted Nash response (Johanson, Zinkevich, Bowling, NIPS 2007, cited via Gan16) [unverified/secondhand].
- Sou05 Bayes' Bluff (Southey et al., UAI 2005) [located, not read].
- LBR: Lisy & Bowling 2017 "Equilibrium approximation quality of current no-limit poker bots" was NOT located; cite as [unverified]. Tim20 (approximate exploitability, learned best response) [abstract-level]. PokaiTrainer uses an "informed local best response" on its solved matrices (Table 9) and notes it does not measure exploitability from omitted actions or leaf-value errors.
- Liu22b safe opponent-exploitation subgame refinement (NeurIPS 2022) [no PDF]; Jea23 safe exploitation for epsilon-equilibria; Li26j (CS-RNR, arXiv 2607.28520) [abstract]: certificate computed on the deployed strategy, "6.2x the steady-state gain of a money-verified binary gate while keeping every deployed strategy within budget"; Mil20 exploiting quantal opponents.
- Li24e (Li, Handina, Ren, Wierman, arXiv 2411.07679) [PDF-read]: Bayesian normal-form and stochastic games, Pareto tradeoff between "opportunity" and "risk" for trusting learned type beliefs; Thm 3.1 existence: misses (1-lambda)(mu-nu) opportunity with risk (1-lambda)(mu-nu)+lambda*mu*eta(Theta); Thm 3.2 impossibility; Cor 3.1 tight: (1-lambda)alpha opportunity, (1+lambda)alpha risk; stochastic version Thms 4.1-4.2 with discount gamma. Uses L1 distances and hypothesis-set diameter eta(Theta); **no Hausdorff distance**. Closest theoretical template for a "blend Nash with exploit" guarantee.
- Policy regret: Aro12b (Arora, Dekel, Tewari 2012), Aro18 (Policy regret in repeated games) [abstracts, not read]. Dig21 (DiGiovanni & Tewari, LAFF) [abstract]: "sublinear regret uniformly over the possible opponents, except exploitative ones" -- regret plus non-exploitability.

### 2.6 Why mixing hurts vs fixed opponents
- Gan16 quote above (Rock-Paper-Scissors example, "put additional weight on paper beyond the equilibrium value of 1/3") [PDF-read]. Basic fact: against a fixed (non-adaptive) opponent the optimal play is a pure best response; any strict mixture over actions of unequal value loses value, and a minimax strategy guarantees only the value. I found no single "canonical" citation beyond Gan15/Gan16; also Ismail "Maximin equilibrium" (Ism19) and old maximin vs Nash literature (Wit93, Hol92, Pru03) surfaced but I did not read them [unverified, tangential].

## 4. New research directions (no-regret focus)

For each: question / why promising / closest prior work / experiment on our harness. Feasibility estimates are mine.

1. **Warm-started and predictive per-turn solvers across turns.**
   - Q: does reusing the previous turn's regrets/strategy (shifted to the new matrix) cut the iterations needed (e.g. 100 -> 10-20) at equal in-play accuracy? Does a predictive RM+ using the previous matrix's solution as the prediction help?
   - Why: consecutive Pokemon matrices are highly correlated (same actives, one turn of change); RM+ is already fast, so the gain is compute for deeper search/larger menus.
   - Prior: Bro16 strategy-based warm starting [abstract-level], Bro14 regret transfer; Far23 and Sul26 (predictive RM+); Car19 (competing against equilibria with evolving payoffs, [abstract-level]).
   - Experiment: log per-turn matrices from the 20k-decision set, solve with cold RM+, warm RM+, PRM+/IR-PRM+; report exploitability of the matrix game vs iteration, and game-level play vs cold RM+ at the same iteration budget.

2. **Agent-form regret minimisation over belief-indexed strategies across turns (Bayesian-game CFR).**
   - Q: does solving the multi-turn Bayesian game (belief as part of the state, opponent per-world strategy) with CFR-style regrets, rather than re-solving each turn with a value net, avoid the myopic information-value failure and fix the "zero value of information at depth 1" problem?
   - Why: PokaiTrainer does this with a trained network; a lightweight depth-limited version using a heuristic value-vector would test whether belief-indexed values alone recover the missing value of concealment.
   - Prior: Yu26d, Bro20 (ReBeL), Sch21, Bro18 (depth-limited solving), Kov19b, Kub23.
   - Experiment: replace scalar leaf with a per-world value vector (from rollouts per world); measure whether a team that conceals information gets nonzero information value and whether win rate improves vs the depth-2 agent.

3. **No-regret opponent modelling with a safety guarantee ("regret-gated exploitation").**
   - Q: can we play a mix of equilibrium and Bayesian best response to a posterior, with the mix weight set by a regret-based confidence test, so that worst-case loss against an adaptive opponent is bounded while we keep the MCTS-type gains (0.58 vs 0.44)?
   - Why: our own finding says the best strategy depends on opponent adaptivity; safety frameworks give the missing bound.
   - Prior: Gan15, Gan16, Joh09, Li26j (confidence-scheduled RNR), Li24e (risk/opportunity Pareto), Liu22b, Dig21.
   - Experiment: opponents = MCTS (non-adaptive), an LBR opponent computed on our solved matrices (as in Yu26d Table 9), and a self-play copy; sweep the restricted-response weight p, plot win rate vs MCTS against win rate vs LBR (the empirical Pareto front), compare with the Li24e bound shape.

4. **Regret-based selection (meta-learner) between Nash, argmax and best-response policies.**
   - Q: can a Hedge/RM over a small set of experts (equilibrium, argmax-of-equilibrium, maximin, BR-to-posterior) across turns of a match (or across matches vs the same opponent) achieve sublinear external regret against the best fixed expert while retaining a security level?
   - Why: matches in Pokemon are short; the right unit is the expert set, not actions. Policy regret caveat: the opponent may adapt (Aro12b, Aro18), so external regret may be misleading.
   - Prior: Dig21 (LAFF: adaptability plus non-exploitability), Aro18, Ber13 (opportunistic strategies for generalized no-regret problems) [abstract-level].
   - Experiment: simulate 50-100 matches vs MCTS opponents with different adaptivity, track cumulative regret to the best expert in hindsight, compare to a fixed Nash agent.

5. **delta_H-aware sampling and selection of determinizations (adaptive world set).**
   - Q: can we choose which determinizations (hidden team sets) to include, or compress worlds by merging those with small Hausdorff distance, to minimise the blend loss bound under a compute budget, and learn which decisions are in the heavy tail (max 22.6% loss)?
   - Why: the heavy tail is where PIMC blending fails; cheap delta_H computed from a few worlds could predict when to fall back to the full agent-form LP.
   - Prior: Lon10 (properties predicting PIMC success), Arj24 (postponing the resolution as a different axis), Whi11/Cow12 (ensemble determinization, [unverified]).
   - Experiment: on the 20k decisions, train a predictor of loss from delta_H and from the Lon10 style properties; evaluate a hybrid policy (blend when predicted loss < tau, LP otherwise) for win rate at equal compute.

6. **Last-iterate and stable regret-matching variants for repeated play and sampling-based execution.**
   - Q: when we sample actions from the solver's output (as the ladder agent does), does the last iterate matter more than the average? Do IR-PRM+ / ExRM+ / SPRM+ give better last-iterate play than RM+ at 20-100 iterations, and does that beat OMWU after tuning its step size?
   - Prior: Cai23, Cai24, Far23, Sul26, Zha25c, Das18, Lee21.
   - Experiment: per-decision matrices as above; metrics are last-iterate duality gap and sampled-play win rate at 10, 20, 50, 100 iterations; include a tuned OMWU as a fair baseline (our OMWU number may be step-size limited).

7. **MMD / magnetic regularisation toward a search or policy prior.**
   - Q: use MMD with a magnet equal to the search-derived or learned policy prior as a drop-in solver that gives a smooth, exploitability-controlled mix between "stick with prior" and equilibrium, as a principled alternative to argmax or sampling temperature.
   - Why: gives a single knob (alpha) interpolating between a pure policy and a regularised equilibrium (QRE-like), with last-iterate convergence; relates directly to the sampling-vs-argmax trade-off.
   - Prior: Sok22 (magnet, last-iterate, "piKL ... suggests MMD may also perform well in such a setting" for planning, not experimentally tested there), Cen21, Per22 (R-NaD), Kub23.
   - Experiment: sweep alpha; report win rate vs MCTS and exploitability vs LBR on the solved matrices; compare with temperature sampling of the RM+ solution.

8. **Certified blend and value-of-information bounds from belief-space structure.**
   - Q: v(b) is concave in the prior b for the maximiser (Aumann-Maschler-type structure; my background knowledge, unverified here). Can we compute a per-decision certificate of the information value (and of the loss from not concealing) from the concavity gap and Lipschitz constants, to decide when concealment/information-gathering moves matter?
   - Prior: Wig15 (structure of the value function of zero-sum games of incomplete information) [metadata only]; Mon96 (proximity of information) [title]; Pon80.
   - Experiment: compute v(b) along belief paths on logged decisions and measure concavity gap against actual win-rate differences between the oracle, agent-form and blend agents.

## 4b. Cross-reference of RESULTS.md conclusions

Source: `/Users/biofanat/Documents/classes/f26/cs535/final/engine/RESULTS.md` (2026-11-13 numbers: agent-form vs PIMC 0.585 [0.540, 0.630], 400 games; bound held on 46,890 decisions, max loss/bound 0.098). Status codes: SUPPORTS / ANTICIPATES / CONTRADICTS / NONE FOUND. Cite keys match the BibTeX block. "PDF-read" caveats of section 1 still apply: spot-check quotes before submission.

**Is the 4*delta_H bound known?** Not found. Lon10 states no loss bound (PDF-read); Arj24 proves only that the count of fusion-creating infostates is monotone in lookahead depth (Props. 1-3, PDF-read); Li24e gives L1/diameter bounds on risk and opportunity under wrong type beliefs (closest analogue, no Hausdorff distance); Yu26 reports no bound. **Caution, my own quick derivation (not verified; send to `verifier`/`prove`):** let v_j be the world-j value with optimal x_j, blend x = sum_j b_j x_j, and delta the max Hausdorff distance (sup-norm) between column sets of any two worlds. Then for every world k and every column c in C_k, x.c >= sum_j b_j (min_{C_j} x_j.c' - delta) = sum_j b_j v_j - delta (pick for each j a column of C_j within delta of c). Hence blend value >= sum_j b_j v_j - delta, and since the agent-form value is <= sum_j b_j v_j (full-information relaxation), blend loss <= delta. I get a constant 1 (a looser 2 or 4 if the per-world deviations are compared pairwise), so the 4 in RESULTS.md may be a loose-but-valid constant, which fits "loose by a factor of 10 or more" (Fig. 3). Treat as an elementary Lipschitz argument: claim the empirical validation and the diagnostic use, not a deep theorem. A check on the proof's exact constants should precede any statement in the paper.

## 4c. Directions grounded in the specific RESULTS.md numbers

These sharpen section 4 using what we measured. Numbering follows section 4 where it overlaps.

A. **delta_H-gated agent-form (RESULTS.md Next experiment 4; sec. 4 item 5).** Facts: loss is concentrated (median 0.19%, 27.7% of decisions >= 1%, 0.7% >= 10%) and the top move differs in 14.9% of decisions (median loss 1.33% vs 0.06% otherwise). Question: how well do delta_H (or a cheaper proxy: max pairwise column-set distance, or disagreement of per-world top moves) predict "top move differs"/loss >= 1%? Closest prior: Lon10 (a priori predictors of PIMC suitability), Arj24, Bro17 (selective re-solving). Experiment: reuse `E5_decisions.jsonl`; ROC of delta_H for the 14.9% flag; then play blend when predicted loss is small and the LP otherwise at a fixed time budget; report score vs mean ms per move relative to always-LP (0.585 at about 340 ms). Because the bound is loose by 10x or more, calibrate a threshold on logged data rather than using 4*delta_H directly; the constant-1 derivation above suggests tightening to 1*delta_H is worth a proof check.

B. **Exploitability E6 and regret-gated exploitation (sec. 4 items 3-4).** Facts: top-move play beats MCTS by about 0.14 over sampling, but the mixed equilibrium is the only robust choice vs adaptive opponents. Experiment: replicate Yu26 Table 9: local best response on our solved matrices (informed) against each of top move, maximin, sampled equilibrium, agent-form; report the LBR win rate/in-model gain per decision. Then the gated mix: play top move with weight p, sampled equilibrium with 1-p, choose p by a restricted-response or confidence test [Joh09, Li26j]; trace the empirical Pareto front (win rate vs MCTS against win rate vs LBR) next to the Li24e shape. Caveat from Yu26: LBR on solved matrices does not measure error from omitted actions or leaf-value error (depth-2 heuristic units here).

C. **Closing the oracle gap with belief-indexed regret (sec. 4 items 2, 8).** Facts: oracle - agent-form about 0.1 in both play modes; hidden info limited to move/Tera reveals, no item/ability inference. Question: how much of the 0.1 is (i) belief error vs (ii) the fundamental cost of hidden information (which agent-form already optimises)? Decompose with an oracle-belief agent-form (true sets known to the belief but the opponent not revealing them) vs the current belief. Prior: Yu26 (belief summary +8-12 pp), Bro20, Pon80/Wig15 (value is concave in the prior; gap between agent-form with true b and with estimated b is controlled by Lipschitz-in-b constants, my inference). Experiment: agent-form with the true posterior over sets vs a prior-only belief vs current belief; add item/ability inference and see the gap close.

D. **Warm-start and last-iterate solvers at 100 iterations (sec. 4 items 1, 6).** Facts: RM+ 100 iterations = LP in play (0.501 vs 0.504); a matrix costs 61 ms and the agent-form solve is the 340 ms per move with 4 worlds. Question: with 4 worlds x per-turn LP, can RM+ with warm start (previous turn's regrets projected) give the agent-form value at 10-20 iterations, making agent-form cheaper than PIMC? Prior: Bro16, Far23, Sul26. Experiment: log matrices from E5; compare the iterations needed to reach LP-equivalent play for cold vs warm RM+/PRM+; also tuned OMWU as a fair baseline.

E. **Depth-3 and belief-indexed leaves (sec. 4 item 2).** Facts: depth-2 matrices with midpoint maximin leaf; value of information structurally zero at depth 1 with scalar leaf. Question: with per-world leaf-value vectors (Yu26 Eq. 1 style), does concealment get a positive value in the agent-form game, and does the C1 advantage grow? Experiment: depth-2 vs depth-3 agent-form vs PIMC; leaf-value vector from per-world rollouts.

F. **MMD-regularised solves toward a prior (sec. 4 item 7)** as a smooth interpolant between top move and equilibrium: alpha sweep, scored against MCTS (non-adaptive) and LBR (adaptive), a continuous version of the 0.14 trade-off.
