## 0. Answers in brief

1. **Regret-based SM-MCTS after Lisy 2013.** The decisive follow-up is Kov15b (already in your notes); the new things are its stated open problems and the 2025 "Simultaneous AlphaZero" (Bec25), which uses regret matching as node solver in a simultaneous-move Markov game with learned values and proves a finite-iteration value-error propagation bound plus an asymptotic tree guarantee. No paper I found gives a finite-time rate for RM/Exp3 selection in SM-MCTS (Kov15b Sec 8.1: "a practically useful bound on this speed of convergence is missing"). Yu24c (NN-CCE) uses EXP-IX not RM/UCB and is a preprint with no convergence theorem for the full method: weak citation.
2. **Depth-limited solving.** Applies to us: Kov19b (value functions; chance nodes yes; simultaneous moves via sequentialization with hidden first move), Sch21 (Theorem 2 gives an explicit exploitability bound with a value-error term over D re-solving steps), Bec25 (gamma^D contraction of frontier error, simultaneous Markov games), Mil21/Mil25 (depth-limited best-response and restricted-Nash with explicit value-error terms). Does not apply: Kov19b has **no** quantitative value-error to exploitability theorem (only an empirical Huber-loss correlation); Sch21 never treats simultaneous moves explicitly; Kub26b safety is for the gadget game only.
3. **Stage solvers.** PRM+ is much faster than RM+/CFR+/DCFR on non-poker games and a 2x2 matrix (Far20), but the T^-1 rate is **unproved** (Far20 Sec 8; Ngu26k and Cai23 still list Alt-PRM+ as open). For a plain matrix game the provable fast options are IR-PRM+/HPRM+ variants (Zha25c, Ngu26k, Sul26) with O(1/T) average and O(T^-1/2) best-iterate. No paper I found reports wall-clock speedups on small *single* matrix games; speedups are in iterations on EFG benchmarks (Far20 Fig 2, Zha25c Fig 4, Ngu26k Table/figs). Kes26: for regret-matching variants the **average** beats the current iterate everywhere (Kuhn, Leduc); PCFR+ current iterate is the exception on Kuhn.
4. **Exploitability lower bounds per unit compute.** LBR (Lis16b) is the cheapest but can be uninformative (full-card bot: full BR finds 90 mBB/h, LBR loses 536 mBB/h). ABR (Tim20b) reaches 89-100% of exact BR on small games but used ~128 TPUv4 chips + 4 TPUv2. Search-based depth-limited BR (CDBR, Mil21) reaches 96-100% of exact-BR gain at lookahead 3-5 on Leduc and Goofspiel vs a uniform-random opponent, but is non-monotone in depth. [inference] For us (exact simulator, per-turn matrix solver) a CDBR-style depth-limited exact-matrix BR is the best compute/tightness trade, with LBR-style greedy BR as a cheap sanity floor and a PPO/ABR as a hard-compute ceiling. No paper directly compares the three at equal compute.

## 2. Extracted facts and quotes (with location)

### 2.1 Regret-based selection in SM-MCTS
- Bec25 [PDF-read] Sec 2.3 p.3: "Hannan consistency is precisely the property a per-node selection rule must satisfy for simultaneous-move MCTS to converge to a subgame-perfect approximate equilibrium ... which is why regret matching, rather than a bandit rule such as UCB, is the natural node solver in this setting." Alg A2 p.19: sigma_i <- RegretMatch(R_i(h)); mu_i <- (1-eps_exp) sigma_i + eps_exp Unif(A_i). Corollary 3 (App A.3, p.15): empirical frequencies are eventually a.s. a subgame-perfect (2D(D+1) eps_exp + xi)-equilibrium. Tables 2-3 p.7: SM-AZ beats SM-MCTS-A by +0.60 +/- 0.01 (Dubins Tag), +2.78 +/- 0.34 (SDA). Sec 5.1: exact regret transfer cuts mean Nash gap 0.251 -> 0.150 over 32 paired trials; advantage reverses by transfer error 0.2.
- Kov15b [PDF-read] Fig 8 / Sec 7.3.1 p.27-28: SM-MCTS vs averaged SM-MCTS-A reach similar distance; averaging "generally slower", up to ~10x iterations for Exp3 on Anti. Fig 9: random depth-6 games, mean final error ~0.25, always <0.3 (eps=0.2, Exp3). Fig 7 / Sec 7.2.1: removing exploration samples gives a "substantial practical improvement".
- Lis13 [PDF-read] Sec 5.1 p.7: "RMM converges slightly slower in all cases"; Sec 5.2 p.8: RM variants reach the minimal achievable 0.0119 / 0.0367 after 10^6 iterations (equals exploitability of the optimal strategy mixed with uniform exploration 0.05).
- No empirical RM+-vs-UCB1-vs-Exp3 comparison after 2013 beyond Tak14 (DUCT best "despite its theoretical shortcomings", abstract-level, from links_attribution) was found; Bec25 compares only SM-MCTS-A vs value-guided variants.

### 2.2 Depth-limited solving
- Kov19b [PDF-read] Theorem 3 (Sec 4.2.2 p.26): "Reachably-optimal value functions preserve Nash equilibria." Prop 4.20 (Sec 4.2.3 p.29): for counterfactually optimal values "DL-CFR can be viewed an instance of CFR-D and inherits its guarantees ... lim expl = 0. In general, the same is not true for reachably-optimal value functions." Chance: yes (model has a chance player, Sec 2 p.4). Simultaneous moves: footnote 35 p.33 "the simultaneous decision is equivalent to player one choosing first and player two choosing second, without learning the first player's choice". Empirical only: Sec 6.2 p.43 "training Huber loss below 0.001 and exploitability below 0.01 ... on par with CFR+ that runs on the full tree" (Goofspiel, Leduc, Oshi-Zumo).
- Sch21 [PDF-read] Theorem 2 (pp.11-12): exploitability of the continually re-solved policy at most (5D+2)(F eps + N U sqrt(A/T)) with D re-solving steps, frontier size F, interior size N, U value-difference bound, A actions, eps-noisy frontier values; assumes public states are never removed. Chance nodes are in the model (p.2). Simultaneous moves: not treated explicitly (the phrase "simultaneous updates" p.9 refers to the CFR update). Results: SoG(400,1) 55% vs PimBot with 10M simulations (Fig 5, p.9); 7 +/- 3 mbb/h vs Slumbot, 434 +/- 9 vs LBR (Table 2, p.8).
- Bec25 [PDF-read] Theorem 3 (App A.1, p.10): ||T^D V - V*||_inf <= gamma^D ||V - V*||_inf (depth-D exact minimax backup contracts frontier error). Theorem 2 (Sec 4.3 p.6): finite-iteration fitted-minimax-VI bound with eps_mg (search) and eps_fit terms, eps_mg = Delta_tree(D eps_exp + eta). Sec 5.1 p.6: "error in regret transfer dominates the effect of leaf value error."
- Mil21 [PDF-read] Theorem E.2 (App E p.13): CDBR utility vs the fixed model plus accumulated regret and boundary value error terms |S| eps_R + sum |I_B^S| eps_V is at least the NE utility; "not an exploitability upper bound". Theorem 5.3 (Sec 5 p.6): E(sigma_R) <= G p/(1-p) + sum |I_S^O|(1-p) eps_V + |S| eps_R + sum |I_S^B| eps_V.
- Kub26b [PDF-read] Theorem 2 (p.4) existence of a GGSE no worse in min counterfactual BR values; Sec 8.1 p.7 nested depth-limited tests on Goofspiel 7 and Liar's dice 2,4 are head-to-head win rates, "only a limited proxy for exploitability".

### 2.3 Stage solvers
- Far20 [PDF-read] Sec 6: prediction m_t = previous loss, m_1 = 0. Sec 7 (printed p.5368): "We conclude that PCFR+ is much faster than CFR+ and DCFR on non-poker games, whereas on poker games DCFR is the fastest." "On Battleship and Pursuit-evasion, PCFR+ is faster than the other algorithms by 3-6 orders of magnitude already after 500 iterations, and around 10 orders of magnitude after 2000 iterations." Intro: "across 18 common zero-sum extensive-form benchmark games ... sometimes by two or more orders of magnitude." "Every algorithm pays almost exactly the same cost per iteration." A 2x2 matrix game is in Fig 2[F]: PCFR+ very fast; RM-based methods slower than T^-1. Prediction-quality dependence: Goofspiel error ~1e-5 gives 0.5-1 order speedup; River endgame error ~1e-3 gives no gain.
- Cai23 [PDF-read] Sec 3 p.5: RM+, alternating RM+, PRM+ keep duality gap ~1e-1 after 1e5 iterations on the 3x3 game, "while alternating PRM+ enjoys good last-iterate convergence"; Sec 6 p.10: on random instances "alternating PRM+ outperforms all other algorithms"; small matrix game: alternating PRM+, ExRM+, SPRM+ reach machine precision after 1e3 iterations.
- Ngu26k [PDF-read] Theorem 7.5 (p.15): Alt-IR-PRM+ and Alt-HPRM+ have O(T^-1/2) best-iterate and last iterate converges to a single NE; average-iterate O(1/T) via Props 5.3, 5.9, 5.13; Alt-RM+ only O(T^-1/2) known (Sec 5, p.6). Sec 9.1 p.17: half prediction (lambda=1/2) gives T^-1 average decay vs ~T^-1/2 for lambda in {1e-4, 1e-2, 1/3}. No matrix-game results table (figures only).
- Zha25c [PDF-read] Sec 7 p.15: tested DCFR, PRM+, AdOGD, IR-PRM+ (as PCFR+ / IR-PCFR+) on the Farina 3x3 counterexample, Liar's dice, Kuhn, Leduc, Goofspiel (4 cards), Battleship (2x3). "There is no algorithm that is consistently best across all games." No OMWU baseline, no wall-clock numbers. "IR-PCFR+ ... is therefore slower per iteration in practice."
- Bur18 [PDF-read]: no measured speedups; Observation 1 p.433: after 2T alternating updates both players can have zero regret yet average strategy exploitability 0.5, so regret does not bound exploitability under alternation without the improvement term (Theorem 1).
- Kes26 [PDF-read] Result 1 p.7: "For every regret-matching variant (CFR, CFR+, LCFR, DCFR, ECFR) the average is strictly less exploitable than the current iterate, on both games and at every recorded horizon." Table 3-4: Kuhn PCFR+ current < 1e-15 (average 1e-7); Leduc DCFR average 4.97e-4, CFR+ average 9.26e-4, PCFR+ current 2.23e-2 vs average 1.95e-3. Table 5: alternating vs simultaneous changes average exploitability by 1-2 orders of magnitude (Kuhn LCFR 1.83e-5 vs 3.73e-3). Recommendation p.9: "report the average strategy unless a predictive method and a convergence check justify the current iterate." Only two small games, single configs.

### 2.4 Exploitability measurement
- Lis16b [PDF-read] p.3: "it computes a lower bound on the exploitability of strategies. Since LBR actually plays a legal poker strategy, it can never win more in expectation than the worst case opponent." p.5: "The results on this bot (Table 3) show that LBR does not realize the actual best response and can even be substantially beaten (i.e., an uninformative lower bound approximation)." ## 3. Open questions and future directions stated by each paper (theory focus)

Verbatim, [PDF-read], location in parentheses.

**Kov15b (SM-MCTS selection)**
- Conjecture, Sec 4.3 p.14: "We conjecture that guaranteed convergence of SM-MCTS might still be possible, provided that the algorithms used as selection policies were HC and that the strategies prescribed by them changed slowly enough - such as is the case with Exp3."
- Problem 5.17 (Sec 5.3 p.22): "Does Theorem 5.4 (and Theorem 5.12) hold with C = kD for some k > 0? If not, what is the optimal value of C?" and "We hypothesize that the answer is affirmative (and possibly the values k = 3, resp. k = 2 after exploration removal, are optimal)". Lower bound 2D eps (Table 1 p.30); upper bounds 2D(D+1) eps and [12(2^D-1)-8D] eps.
- UPO unproved, Sec 5.2 p.19: "While we were unable to prove that specific eps-HC algorithms have the eps-UPO property, ... we provide empirical evidence" for regret matching and Exp3; Sec 5.2.1 p.20: UPO "is quite impractical ... we were unable to find a replacement property which would be easier to check".
- Sec 8.1 p.30: "a practically useful bound on this speed of convergence is missing"; "it would be interesting to better understand the UPO property and, more generally, the propagation of information and uncertainty in game trees with simultaneous moves"; "These could be further improved by allowing the exploration rate to depend on the position in the game tree."
- Scope limits: chance nodes are omitted "purely to reduce the amount of technical notation ... This can be proven in a straightforward way" (footnote 2 p.4); selection algorithms assumed not to share non-local variables (Sec 2.2 p.6); observed error "even the linear bound ... is too pessimistic" (Sec 7.3.2 p.27).

**Lis13**: "the rate of the convergence is not given" (Sec 5.2 p.7); "we will focus our future research on providing finite time convergence bounds for these algorithms and generalizing the results to more general classes of extensive-form games with imperfect information" (Sec 6 p.8); "We conjecture that many of these algorithms also converge to eps-Nash equilibrium and that our formal analysis could be extended" (p.2); proof needs mean-propagation but practice uses current-sample (Sec 5.1 p.7).

**Bec25**: theory covers SM-AZ-mu (fixed-distribution sampling), not the self-play SM-AZ used empirically (Sec 4 p.4); "the theory assumes fixed-distribution sampling and asymptotic search, and the experiments use single trained models" (Conclusion p.7). No explicit future-work list; the search-level guarantee only asserts the existence of t_0 (Cor 3).

**Far20 (PRM+)**: Sec 8 (printed p.5369): "Can PRM+ guarantee T^-1 convergence on matrix games like optimistic FTRL and OMD, or do the less stable updates prevent that? Can one develop a predictive variant of DCFR, which is faster on poker domains? Can one combine DCFR and PCFR+, so DCFR would be faster initially but PCFR+ would overtake?" Far20 does not analyse last-iterate.

**Cai23**: "Despite its strong practical performance, it is unknown if alternating PRM+ enjoys ergodic convergence" (Sec 2 p.4); "understanding the good practical performance of alternating PRM+ (an algorithm for which neither ergodic nor last-iterate convergence guarantees are known) remains open" (Sec 6 p.10); "Several questions remain open, including giving concrete rates for (non-restarted) ExRM+, SPRM+, and alternating PRM+" and "Another open problem is understanding if our results extend to the case of solving EFGs with RM+ via the CFR framework" (Sec 7 p.10); the strict-NE hypothesis "cannot be weakened to ... a unique, non-strict NE" (Sec 3 p.5).

**Ngu26k**: "no general guarantee is known for its average-, best-, or last-iterate convergence" for Alt-PRM+ (Intro p.1); update order question: "whether alternating updates merely accelerate convergence or can determine whether the strategy profile approaches equilibrium at all" (p.2), answered only for HPRM+ on one game (Theorem 8.1: Sim-HPRM+ liminf gap >= 1/sqrt(3), Alt-HPRM+ converges); "whether analogous separations occur for lambda > 1/2 under alternating self-play remains unresolved" (Sec 9.1 p.18); "Any O(T^-1) guarantee for another fixed multiplier across all finite games must exploit additional structure of self-play" (Sec 6 p.13); numerical evidence "does not establish asymptotic lower bounds or last-iterate nonconvergence" (p.18).

**Zha25c**: Sec 8 p.17 questions: "What properties can be proven about the alternating variants of these algorithms, especially PCFR+?"; "Does IR-PRM+ have a best-iterate and/or O(1/T) convergence rate when used without the extra-gradient setup?"; "Can one show a poly(m,n)/T average-iterate convergence rate ... Our current bounds depend on several game-dependent constants"; "Is linear last-iterate theoretically guaranteed for any or all of these algorithms?" and (Sec 7 p.15) linear last-iterate behaviour "is unexplained theoretically, especially in extensive-form games". Mystery framing (Sec 8): "There has long been a mystery about why RM+ performs so well in practice".

**Kov19b (depth-limited values)**: "Problem A.2. Is the set of (counterfactually) optimal value functions convex?" (App A p.52); fictitious play: "we defer the analysis of its depth-limited variants to a future work" (Sec 4.2.3 p.30); "A limitation of Proposition 4.24 is its non-constructiveness" (fn 32 p.33); no formal value-error to exploitability bound (empirical Huber 0.001 -> expl 0.01, Sec 6.2 p.43); training coverage: CFR-D data "contains problematic ranges which are not covered by the training data" (App E.2 p.81); experiments only on medium domains (Sec 6 p.38).

**Sch21**: theory limited to two-player zero-sum (p.2); guarantee holds "so long as the value function is reasonably accurate" (p.6); Theorem 1/2 assume "public states are never removed from the search tree" (p.11); enumerating infostates per public state "can be prohibitively expensive" (Discussion p.8); training convergence "with very large (exponential) memory" (p.12).

**Mil21**: "Constant-size gadget fulfilling the Definition 5.1 is an open problem" (Sec 5 p.5); sound CDRNR complexity "quadratic in the depth of the game" (Sec 5 p.5); "we observe a lack of monotonicity in step increase" (Sec 6 p.7) and CDBR "can perform worse than a Nash equilibrium against the fixed opponent because of the perfect opponent assumption after the depth-limit" (App F p.14); Theorem 5.3 bound "is very loose in practice ... rarely reaches even a tenth of the gain" (Sec 6 pp.6-7).

**Tim20b (ABR)**: no theorem on how loose the lower bound is or at what rate it tightens (my reading; the paper states only the lower-bound property, Sec 3 p.3489); "does not converge to the exact best response in the limit [Lisy et al., 2015]" for plain IS-MCTS (Sec 3.2 p.3489); limited to "tractable belief spaces" (Sec 5 p.3492), possibly relaxed "by using a sample model"; "replacing the exact opponent policy with a different policy weakens the quality of the resulting BR" (Sec 3.2 p.3490); "practitioners much [sic] decide how practical worst-case scenarios are" (Sec 4.2 p.3492).

**Lis16b (LBR)**: no formal future-work list; greedy one-step look-ahead "would substantially increase its computational requirements" if extended (p.3); tightness failure on the full-card bot (p.5).

## 4. Open theory questions we could attack

Setting: per-turn RM+/Hedge agent in a finite-horizon zero-sum stochastic game with simultaneous moves (stage matrix = one simulated turn + heuristic leaf), plus an agent-form hidden-information solve over K sampled worlds. Ranked by fit (1 = best). "First result" is my suggestion ([inference]).

1. **Value-error and solver-error propagation with a simultaneous-move min-max step, finite horizon, chance, with a matching lower bound.**
   - Gap: Kov19b has only an empirical error-to-exploitability link; Sch21 Theorem 2 has a (5D+2) factor with frontier/interior size constants; Bec25 Theorem 2/3 gives gamma^D contraction and fitted-VI bounds for discounted Markov games; Per15 (in earlier notes) is discounted. Our own H-bar*(eps_s + 2 eps_v) bound is the finite-horizon analogue.
   - First result: (a) a clean theorem exploitability <= sum_t E[eps_s,t] + 2 sum_t E[eps_v,t] with trajectory-dependent weights (our L6), (b) a **tightness example** (a chain game where the 2*eps_v*H-bar is attained, or where it is provably sqrt-type smaller), which none of Sch21/Bec25/Kov19b gives. Then test it on logged Pokemon stage matrices as a diagnostic (compare to measured exploitability of the heuristic leaf).
2. **Last-iterate vs average-iterate guarantee of RM+ / PRM+ for a single small stage matrix at T about 100, plus warm-start across turns.**
   - Gap: RM+ average iterate O(T^-1/2) only (Ngu26k Sec 5); RM+ last iterate converges only under strict NE (Cai23 Thm 1); Alt-PRM+ unknown (Cai23, Ngu26k, Zha25c); Kes26 shows empirically average is the safe report.
   - First result: a finite-T bound for the **average** iterate of RM+ started from a regret vector warm-started from the previous turn's matrix, stated in terms of the distance between consecutive matrices (Lipschitz in entries) and showing O(1/sqrt(T)) with a smaller constant when matrices move little; then an empirical test on the 20k logged decisions comparing cold RM+, warm RM+, and IR-PRM+/HPRM+ (Zha25c/Ngu26k are parameter-free). A negative-result variant: a 2x2 or 3x3 stage matrix where playing the last iterate of RM+ is worse than the average by a constant at T=100 (Cai23-style).
3. **Hedge mixing equilibrium and exploit experts with value error: a bound that is not as loose as CDRNR's.**
   - Gap: Mil21 Theorem 5.3 "is very loose in practice ... rarely reaches even a tenth of the gain"; GS15 (links_attribution) dismisses the Hedge route as not safe in finite repeated games; Li24e (notes) is L1-type.
   - First result: finite-sample epsilon_T-safety of a Hedge mixture in a stochastic game (our L2) with slack R(T) + 2 eps_v E[T], plus a tighter instance-dependent form when the exploit expert is a deterministic argmax of the same solved matrix; test vs the Mil21 p/(1-p) gain bound on our E6 exploitability numbers.
4. **Agent-form (single agent strategy over K sampled worlds) error: sampling of worlds, belief error and the blend bound.**
   - Gap: Kov19b Theorem 5 shows public-state-sized information is needed ("information covering less than the whole common-knowledge public state may not suffice"); Sch21 requires infostate enumeration; Kub26b/Zha21-type gadgets have no value-error theorem; Tim20b needs tractable beliefs.
   - First result: a bound on the value gap between the K-world agent-form LP and the full-belief LP as a function of K (concentration of the empirical belief in total variation) times a Lipschitz constant of v in b, combined with our delta_H blend bound; verified on the 46,890 logged decisions. Note the value is concave in the prior b (Aumann-Maschler, from your notes, unverified).
5. **Exploitability lower bounds from search-based best responses: tightness and monotone-in-depth guarantees.**
   - Gap: Tim20b states only "lower bound ... with equality when the approximation matches the exact best response"; Lis16b gives a counterexample (LBR -536 vs BR 90 mBB/h); Mil21 shows CDBR is non-monotone in depth and can be worse than NE vs the model (App F).
   - First result: for a depth-d CDBR with leaf error eps_v, a two-sided certificate value_d - 2 d eps_v-type <= true BR value <= ... ; or a "best-of-depths" estimator that is monotone by construction (max over depths and over fixed-opponent policies) and an example family where plain depth-d is non-monotone. Fits our E6 plan (LBR on solved matrices) directly; first empirical step: report BR value as an interval across depths.
6. **Regret-matching selection in SM-MCTS: finite-time rate and the UPO property (the opponent MCTS baseline, and our depth-2 matrix as a one-level tree).**
   - Gap: Kov15b Problem 5.17, "slowly changing" conjecture (p.14), UPO unproved for RM and Exp3, no finite-time rate (Sec 8.1); Lis13 same; Bec25 asymptotic only.
   - First result: for **depth-1** (our case: a single matrix with a fixed leaf value) prove UPO and a finite-time (eps + O(1/sqrt N)) bound for RM/RM+ selection when leaf payoffs are fixed in advance; the depth-2 extension through the "slowly changing" argument is the harder step. Lower fit because our agent does not run MCTS; value is as an explanation of why the MCTS baseline plays near-equilibrium or not.
7. **RM+ on the agent-form game as an EFG (per-world opponent regret tables) without full-reach equilibria.**
   - Gap: Cai23 Sec 7 (extension of RM+ results to EFGs via CFR is open); Zha25c Sec 8 ("Our convergence results in extensive-form games hinge on having a full-reach Nash equilibrium"); Zha25c Sec 6 constants include 1/min reach probability.
   - First result: show that the agent-form game (agent strategy shared, opponent per-world) is a two-level matrix game (not a deep EFG), so the matrix-game IR-PRM+ O(1/T) average rate applies directly with the world-weighted payoff, avoiding the full-reach assumption. Cheap to state; modest novelty.
8. **Does update order (alternating vs simultaneous) matter for the stage solver's output?**
   - Gap: Ngu26k answered "yes" for one HPRM+ game only; Kes26 saw 1-2 orders of magnitude on Kuhn/Leduc; Bur18 gives the proof but no numbers.
   - First result: empirical map on our stage matrices (alternating vs simultaneous RM+ at T=10, 20, 100, last vs average), plus a Cai23-style counterexample search over 3x3 payoffs from Pokemon-like structure. Lowest theoretical fit; high practical value.

## 5. Ranked top 8 to build on

1. **Bec25** (Simultaneous AlphaZero). Only paper with RM node selection, simultaneous moves, Markov games and an explicit finite-iteration value-error bound; Theorem 3 (gamma^D contraction) is the template for our frontier-error bound; regret-transfer sensitivity (Fig 2) is a ready baseline for a warm-start study. Caveat: preprint, discounted, continuous-state domains.
2. **Kov15b**. Theory frame and open problems for RM/Exp3 selection (UPO, rate, constant C); sets what can and cannot be claimed about SM-MCTS opponents.
3. **Kov19b**. Value-function classes for depth-limited solving, chance nodes and simultaneous moves via sequentialization; states clearly that no quantitative error bound exists, which is the hole we fill.
4. **Sch21**. Theorem 2 is the only explicit exploitability-with-value-error bound for continual re-solving; use as comparison for our H-bar bound.
5. **Far20 / Ngu26k / Zha25c** (take as one stage-solver cluster, in this order: Ngu26k for current provable O(1/T) and last-iterate theorems, Far20 for the PRM+ definition and 18-game speedups, Zha25c for scale-invariant, parameter-free IREG-PRM+). Counted as one slot.
6. **Mil21**. Search-based BR with a value function, Theorem E.2, and the safety bound Theorem 5.3 that the Hedge layer should be compared against; non-monotone-in-depth caveat matters for E6.
7. **Tim20b**. Lower-bound property, 89-100% of exact BR on small games, comparison to LBR; use as the "RL BR ceiling" and for citing why our PPO BR is weak.
8. **Lis16b**. Cheapest exploitability floor with the concrete counterexample to tightness (BR 90 vs LBR -536 mBB/h); justifies reporting exploitability as an interval.

Runners-up: Cai23 (strict-NE last-iterate, Alt-PRM+ empirical), Kes26 (average vs current iterate on exact exploitability), Kub26b, Mil25, Bur18, Zha21 (not read).
