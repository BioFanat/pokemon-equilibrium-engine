## 1. Kakade and Langford 2002: performance-difference lemma

**Citation.** S. Kakade, J. Langford. "Approximately Optimal Approximate Reinforcement Learning." ICML 2002, pp. 267-274 [memory for pages]. No DOI. Undermind key Kak02; Semantic Scholar link https://www.semanticscholar.org/paper/523b4ce1c2a1336962444abc1dec215756c2f3e6. PDF: https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~sham/papers/rl/aoarl.pdf. The PDF text layer is garbled (Type 3 fonts), so I read the statement from the rendered page (PDF page 6 of 8).

**Lemma 6.1 [read-pdf img]**: "For any policies pi~ and pi and any starting state distribution mu,
eta_mu(pi~) - eta_mu(pi) = 1/(1-gamma) E_{(a,s)~pi~ d_{pi~}} [A_pi(s,a)]."
The paper's value is normalised: the proof starts "V_pi~(s) = (1-gamma) sum_t gamma^t E[R(s_t,a_t)]" and the lemma follows by telescoping with the advantage of the *old* policy pi, evaluated under the *new* policy pi~'s discounted state distribution d_{pi~,mu}. Theorem 6.2 [read-pdf img] then bounds eta(pi*) - eta(pi) <= eps/(1-gamma) ||d_{pi*,mu~}/d_{pi,mu}||_inf for OPT(A_{pi,mu}) < eps (the original distribution-mismatch bound).

**Relation to our claim.**
- Our L1 Remark 3 writes V* - V^pi = E_pi[sum_t (V*(s_t) - Q*(s_t,a_t))] and calls it "the second form of the PDL (KL02)". KL02 states **one** identity. Put pi~ := pi and pi := pi* in Lemma 6.1 to get our expression (advantage of pi*, i.e. Q* - V*, summed along pi's own trajectory); swap the roles to get the other form. So: **special case (same lemma, roles chosen)**; no "second form" is stated in KL02.
- The undiscounted finite-horizon version with absorbing terminals is the same telescoping with the factor 1/(1-gamma) replaced by the un-normalised occupancy (Dask21 Lemma 3 below states it that way).
- Credit line in Aga21: "Lemma 3.2. (The performance difference lemma [Kakade and Langford, 2002])" [read-pdf text]. Aga21 does not credit Kakade's thesis for it. EKM04 Lemma 7 is called "a variant of a result in Kakade [2003]" [read-pdf img] (average-reward form).

## 2. Even-Dar, Kakade, Mansour: Online MDPs (experts in every state)

**Citation.** E. Even-Dar, S. M. Kakade, Y. Mansour. "Online Markov Decision Processes." Mathematics of Operations Research 34(3):726-736, 2009. DOI 10.1287/moor.1090.0396 (Undermind/Crossref). Conference version: "Experts in a Markov Decision Process." NIPS 17, 2004. **I could not obtain the 2009 PDF; everything below is from the 2004 NIPS version** (`EKM04.pdf`, from https://homes.cs.washington.edu/~sham/papers/rl/experts_mdp.pdf). Theorem numbers may differ in the journal version.

**Setting [read-pdf text]**: finite MDP, transition model P known and fixed, adversarial reward sequence r_1..r_T in [0,1]; learner sees past rewards only; objective "the sum of undiscounted rewards over a T step horizon"; comparator = best *stationary* policy in hindsight. Algorithm: "We associate each state with an experts algorithm", fed the loss Q_{pi_t,r_t}(s,.).

**Assumption 1 (Mixing) [read-pdf text]**: "the transition model over states, as determined by pi, has a well defined stationary distribution... there exists some tau such that for all policies pi, and distributions d and d', ||dP^pi - d'P^pi||_1 <= e^{-1/tau} ||d - d'||_1". Assumption 4 (black-box experts): regret M sqrt(T log|A|) plus a slow-change condition ||q_t - q_{t+1}||_1 <= sqrt(log|A|/t).

**Theorem 5 [read-pdf img]**: "Let A be the MDP experts algorithm. Then for all reward functions r_1..r_T and for all stationary policies pi, V_{r_1..r_T}(A) >= V_{r_1..r_T}(pi) - 8 tau^2 sqrt(log|A|/T) - 3 tau sqrt(log|A|/T) - 4 tau/T." The text adds that the bound "does not depend on the size of the state space". Theorem 6 (idealised): sum_t eta_{r_t}(pi_t) >= sum_t eta_{r_t}(pi) - 3 tau sqrt(T log|A|). Lemma 7 (average-reward PDL): eta_r(pi') - eta_r(pi) = E_{s~d_{pi'}}[Q_{pi,r}(s,pi') - Q_{pi,r}(s,pi)] ("a variant of a result in Kakade [2003]").

**Relation.** Our L2 Remark 1 and L3 are the same "Hedge per state fed with Q" idea. Differences: (i) EKM is a single-agent MDP with adversarial *rewards*, average-reward with mixing time tau as the horizon; ours is a finite-horizon episodic game with absorbing terminals, so H-bar replaces tau, no mixing assumption; (ii) L3 is a *two-player* statement (the opponent faces the MDP M_pi induced by a fixed agent). **Same technique, different setting.** Aga21 says its own NPG proof "borrows ideas from the online regret framework in changing MDPs (in [Even-Dar et al., 2009])" and that "their MDP Experts Algorithm is equivalent to the natural policy gradient algorithm with the softmax policy parameterization" [agent-read, Aga21 p.7 and p.23]. The "in every state" per-state Hedge in L2 Remark 1 is this algorithm.

## 3. Agarwal, Kakade, Lee, Mahajan 2021 (JMLR): NPG with softmax = multiplicative weights

**Citation.** A. Agarwal, S. M. Kakade, J. D. Lee, G. Mahajan. "On the Theory of Policy Gradient Methods: Optimality, Approximation, and Distribution Shift." JMLR 22(98), 2021 (page range not checked; 77 PDF pages). arXiv 1908.00261. (Undermind key Aga19.) PDF page index = printed page.

**Lemma 5.1 (NPG as soft policy iteration) [read-pdf text]**: for the softmax parameterization, NPG updates take the form theta^(t+1) = theta^(t) + eta/(1-gamma) A^(t) and pi^(t+1)(a|s) = pi^(t)(a|s) exp(eta A^(t)(s,a)/(1-gamma)) / Z_t(s). Text right after (p.22): "they are identical to the classical multiplicative weights updates [Freund and Schapire, 1997, Cesa-Bianchi and Lugosi, 2006] for online linear optimization over the probability simplex, where the linear functions are specified by the advantage function of the current policy at each iteration. Notably, there is no dependence on the state distribution d_rho^(t)". The words "Hedge" and "no-regret" do not occur in the paper [agent-read].

**Theorem 5.3 (exact NPG) [read-pdf text]**: "Suppose we run the NPG updates (15) using rho and with theta^(0) = 0. Fix eta > 0. For all T > 0: V^(T)(rho) >= V*(rho) - log|A|/(eta T) - 1/((1-gamma)^2 T)." With eta >= (1-gamma)^2 log|A|, T <= 2/((1-gamma)^2 eps) iterations suffice, "which has no dependence on the number of states or actions". **No distribution-mismatch coefficient** in this rate; the paper says so explicitly (Table 1 caption, Section 5 intro) [agent-read]. The proof [read-pdf text] is: PDL (Lemma 3.2) with the comparator's state distribution d*, rewrite the advantage via the closed-form update as (1/eta)(KL(pi*_s||pi_s^(t)) - KL(pi*_s||pi_s^(t+1)) + log Z_t(s)), telescope. That is exactly the argument in our L3 proof (PDL with fixed comparator weighting, per-state Hedge regret).

**Distribution mismatch coefficient, Definition 3.1 (p.12) [agent-read, matches my text read]**: ||d_rho^pi / mu||_inf. It enters Theorem 4.1 (projected PG), Theorem 5.2 / Corollary 5.1 (log-barrier PG), not Theorem 5.3.

**Inexact NPG.** Section 6 contains Theorems 6.1 (Q-NPG) and 6.2 (NPG), Corollaries 6.1-6.3, Lemma 6.2 ("NPG Regret Lemma") [agent-read inventory; I confirmed Theorem 6.1/6.2 statements in the text]:
- Theorem 6.2 (agnostic NPG) [read-pdf text]: E[min_{t<T} {V^{pi*}(rho) - V^(t)(rho)}] <= (W/(1-gamma)) sqrt(2 beta log|A|/T) + sqrt(kappa eps_stat/(1-gamma)^3) + sqrt(eps_bias)/(1-gamma). Here eps_bias is the **transfer error measured under the comparator's measure d* = d^{pi*}_rho(s) pi*(a|s)** (Assumption 6.5), and kappa is a relative condition number of feature covariances (Assumption 6.2/6.5). So the "mismatch" enters through the *comparator-measure* error eps_bias and kappa, not through ||d*/mu||_inf.
- Corollary 6.1 (Q-NPG) [agent-read, rendered]: replaces the transfer error by an on-policy approximation error and gets a term sqrt(||d*/nu||_inf 4|A| eps_approx/(1-gamma)^3); the proof step is "L(w*;theta,d*) <= ||d*/d^(t)||_inf L(w*;theta,d^(t)) <= (1/(1-gamma)) ||d*/nu||_inf L(...)" . This is the closest analogue of our L3b: critic error under the learner's distribution, transferred to the comparator's distribution by a sup-norm density ratio.

**Relation to L3 / L3b.** L3 = Theorem 5.3's argument, finite-horizon, with the opponent MDP M_pi (the PDL weights are the comparator's un-normalised occupancy, sum = H-bar(sigma_o), which replaces 1/(1-gamma)). L3b = Cor 6.1's transfer step. **Same technique, different setting/constants.**

## 4. Lisy et al. 2013 and Kovarik-Lisy: Hannan-consistent selection in SM-MCTS

### 4a. Lisy, Kovarik, Lanctot, Bosansky (NeurIPS 2013)
**Citation.** V. Lisy, V. Kovarik, M. Lanctot, B. Bosansky. "Convergence of Monte Carlo Tree Search in Simultaneous Move Games." NeurIPS 26, pp. 2112-2120, 2013. arXiv 1310.8613 (Undermind lists only the arXiv DOI 10.48550/arXiv.1310.8613). Read: arXiv v2 [read-pdf text + img of p.6].

- **Def 4.1 [read-pdf img]**: "a player is eps-Hannan-consistent if, for any payoff sequences (e.g., against any opponent strategy), limsup_{t->inf} r(t) <= eps holds almost surely" (r(t) = average external regret vs the best fixed action).
- **Def 4.2**: "guaranteed exploration": lim t_ij = inf a.s. for every joint action. Lemma 4.4: the gamma-uniform-exploration modification A* of an eps-HC algorithm is (eps+gamma)-HC.
- **Lemma 4.6**: if both players in a matrix game of value v are eps-HC, then limsup u(br, sigma^_2(t)) <= v + 2 eps and liminf u(sigma^_1(t), br) >= v - 2 eps (empirical frequencies, a.s.). Cor 4.7: (4 eps + delta)-equilibrium.
- **Theorem 4.11 (main) [read-pdf img, p.6]**: "Let (M^h)_{h in H} be a game with perfect information and simultaneous moves with maximal depth D. Then for every eps-Hannan consistent algorithm A with guaranteed exploration and arbitrary small delta > 0, there almost surely exists t_0, so that the average strategies (sigma^_1(t), sigma^_2(t)) form a subgame perfect (2D^2 + delta) eps-Nash equilibrium for all t >= t_0."
- **Required variant [read-pdf text, Sec. 3]**: "The standard way of defining the value to send back is RetVal(u1, Xh, nh) = u1, but we discuss also RetVal = Xh/nh, which is required for the formal analysis in Section 4." Sec. 5.1: "The formal analysis presented in the previous section requires the algorithms to return the mean of all the previous samples instead of the value of the current sample." The tree is assumed completely built (Sec. 4) and payoffs are in [0,1].
- **No rate [read-pdf text]**: "Although the formal analysis guarantees the convergence to an eps-NE of the game, the rate of the convergence is not given" (Sec. 5.2) [agent-read quote; consistent with the theorem's "almost surely exists t_0"].
- UCB: "Shafiei et al. [17] show that it does not converge to Nash equilibrium, even in a simple one-stage simultaneous move game. In this paper, we focus on variants of MCTS, which provably converge" [read-pdf text, Sec. 1]. Lis13 does not say whether UCB is Hannan consistent.
- Flag: the p.13 proof arithmetic for the constant 2D^2 has an internal inconsistency that the agent noticed ([agent-read] "2D eps + delta at the root vs 2(D-1) eps + delta"). KL18 later states C = 2D(D+1) for its own (different) algorithm. Do not rely on the exact constant "2D^2"; the safe statement is "quadratic in depth".

### 4b. Kovarik and Lisy (Machine Learning)
**Citation.** V. Kovarik, V. Lisy. "Analysis of Hannan consistent selection for Monte Carlo tree search in simultaneous move games." Machine Learning (online 2019; print 2019/2020, volume/pages not checked), DOI 10.1007/s10994-019-05832-z. arXiv 1804.09045 (v3, Jul 2019; an earlier arXiv version is 1509.00149). Undermind lists it with year 2015 under key Kov15b; cite the journal. Read: arXiv v3 [read-pdf text + img].

- **Abstract [read-pdf text]**: "We show that using HC algorithms directly on the observed payoffs is not sufficient to guarantee the convergence. With an additional averaging over joint actions, the convergence is guaranteed, but empirically slower. We further define an additional property of HC algorithms, which is sufficient to guarantee the convergence without the averaging..."
- **Theorem 4.1 [read-pdf img, p.11; the constant is 1/5, rendered and checked by me; one agent report said 1/2, which is a slip]**: "There exists a simultaneous-move zero-sum game G with perfect information and a HC algorithm A, such that when A is used as a selection policy for SM-MCTS, then the average strategy sigma-bar(t) almost surely converges outside of the set of 1/5-Nash equilibria." The algorithm is a constructed HC algorithm with guaranteed exploration, not UCT/UCB. Cause: "payoff observation bias... synchronization of the selection algorithm... with the rewards".
- **Theorem 5.4 [read-pdf text + agent-read img, p.16]**: SM-MCTS-A (selection uses the average joint-action reward, raw values back-propagated) with an eps-HC selector with guaranteed exploration: empirical frequencies eventually arbitrarily close to a subgame-perfect C eps-equilibrium, **C = 2D(D+1)** (quadratic in depth D).
- **Theorem 5.12 [read-pdf img, p.20]**: plain SM-MCTS with an eps-HC, eps-UPO selector (UPO = "unbiased payoff observations", Def 5.11): C' eps-NE with **C' = 12(2^D - 1) - 8D** (exponential in D). Sec. 5.3 [agent-read]: "the bound ... is quadratic, resp. exponential, in the game depth."
- **Lower bound on the constant, Prop 5.15 / Example 5.16 [read-pdf text]**: none of Thms 5.4 and 5.12 hold with C replaced by kD for a suitable k (so at least linear in D is needed). Open Problem 5.17. Table 1 (p.30) [agent-read]: lower bound 2D eps.
- UPO is not proved for any concrete selector: "While we were unable to prove that specific eps-HC algorithms have the eps-UPO property... we provide empirical evidence" [agent-read, p.19].
- **No finite-time rate [agent-read, p.30]**: "a practically useful bound on this speed of convergence is missing."
- On the relation to Lis13 [read-pdf text, Sec. 2.2]: "in our previous work (Lisy et al. 2013) we prove a result similar to Theorem 5.4 here. However, the algorithm that we used earlier is different from SM-MCTS-A... the previous version... updates the selection algorithm based on the averaged values, but then it propagates backward these averaged numbers - and on the next level, it takes averages of averages". KL18 does not say Lis13's bound was loose; the two constants refer to different algorithms.
- Also [read-pdf text, Bosansky et al. AIJ 2016 Thm 4.5, citing the arXiv 1509.00149 version]: "There are games, in which SM-MCTS instantiated by any regret minimizing selection function with a constant exploration gamma cannot converge to a strategy that would be an eps-Nash equilibrium for an eps < gamma D, where D is the depth of the game tree." I did not find this exact theorem number in the v3 text; cite it via Bosansky et al.

**Relation to our L4.** The L4 identity (visit shares' Nash gap = (Reg1+Reg2)/N) is exact for a single node with fixed matrix and is the same regret-to-gap conversion as Lis13 Lemma 4.6 / Blum-Mansour. For a **tree**, our note's sentence is wrong as written: regret-minimising selectors at each node do not suffice (KL18 Thm 4.1). What is true and citable: (a) Lis13 Thm 4.11 for the mean-propagating variant, asymptotic, constant ~D^2; (b) KL18 Thm 5.4 (SM-MCTS-A, C = 2D(D+1)) and Thm 5.12 (UPO, C' exponential in D). None of the three covers UCB1 selection or raw-return back-propagation without UPO (as in poke-engine). Also, neither paper has a leaf-error term: Lis13 builds the complete tree to terminal payoffs in [0,1] (no value function or rollout assumption beyond that; [agent-read] Alg. 1 only has `Rollout`) and KL18 likewise works with terminal utilities in [0,1]. So the frontier-leaf error that Theorem A charges as 2 eps_v (and L5) has no analogue in the SM-MCTS convergence theorems; they are statements about the limit of the full game tree.

## 5. Shafiei-Sturtevant-Schaeffer 2009 and later results on UCT/DUCT

**Citation.** M. Shafiei, N. Sturtevant, J. Schaeffer. "Comparing UCT versus CFR in Simultaneous Games." IJCAI-09 Workshop on General Game Playing (GIGA'09), pp. 75-82, 2009 (venue/pages from Lis13's reference list [read-pdf text]). No DOI. Undermind key Sha09. Full text not obtained.

**What they show.**
- Abstract [abstract; DU summary page https://www.cs.du.edu/~sturtevant/papers/shafiei2009cfr.html]: "We argue that UCT does not converge to a Nash equilibrium in general and the situation that it converges to can be exploited. We also analyze CFR... and show how it can be used to exploit UCT."
- How others describe it [read-pdf text, Bosansky et al. AIJ 2016, `SMjournal.pdf`]: "Shafiei et al. in [14] provide a counter-example showing that this straightforward application of UCT does not converge to an equilibrium even in the simplest simultaneous move games and that a player playing a NE can exploit this strategy" (Sec. 1); "(a well-known counter-example in Rock, Paper, Scissors with biased payoffs [14])" for the mixed (visit-share) form DUCT(mix); "If the algorithm is instantiated by UCT, Shafiei et al. [14] have shown that the algorithm converges to a stable strategy, which is not close to a Nash equilibrium. We replicate the experiment below and note that this is the case only for the deterministic version of UCT. A randomized version of UCT with a well selected exploration parameter empirically converges close to the equilibrium strategy, but then in some games oscillates and does not converge further." Sec. 6.3 reruns it on Biased RPS for 10^8 iterations.
- Game: **Biased Rock-Paper-Scissors** (one-stage matrix game) [Bosansky et al.; and snippet]. Lis13 says "even in a simple one-stage simultaneous move game" [read-pdf text].

**Is there a formal DUCT non-convergence proof?** I did not find one.
- Bosansky et al. AIJ 2016 [read-pdf text]: "none of the variants of this algorithm introduced above has been proven to eventually converge to a Nash equilibrium"; the Shafiei result is a counter-example plus experiment. They also say (the SM-MCTS discussion near their Sec. 4) "The only known theoretical result about SM-MCTS directly applicable to the algorithms in this paper is negative, and it has been proven in [59]" = Kovarik-Lisy (Theorem 4.5 in their numbering, the eps >= gamma D lower bound for regret-minimising selectors; this is not about UCT).
- Tak, Lanctot, Winands, "Monte Carlo Tree Search Variants for Simultaneous Move Games," IEEE CIG 2014, DOI 10.1109/CIG.2014.6932889 [snippet/abstract only]: reports Decoupled UCT performs best overall in nine games "despite its theoretical shortcomings". Not a proof.

## 8. Littman 1994: minimax-Q

**Citation.** M. L. Littman. "Markov games as a framework for multi-agent reinforcement learning." ICML 1994, pp. 157-163 [memory for pages]. DOI 10.1016/b978-1-55860-335-6.50027-1. Read: PDF from https://courses.cs.duke.edu/spring07/cps296.3/littman94markov.pdf.

**[read-pdf img, Figure 1 "The minimax-Q algorithm", p.4]**: Learn: `Q[s,a,o] := (1-alpha)*Q[s,a,o] + alpha*(rew + gamma*V[s'])`; `pi[s,.] := argmax{pi'[s,.], min{o', sum{a', pi[s,a']*Q[s,a',o']}}}` (by linear programming); `V[s] := min{o', sum{a', pi[s,a']*Q[s,a',o']}}`. **[read-pdf text]**: "This algorithm is called minimax-Q since it is essentially identical to the standard Q-learning algorithm with a minimax replacing the max." So the minimax Bellman operator (Shapley's operator on Q) is V(s) = max_pi min_o sum_a pi_a Q(s,a,o), Q(s,a,o) = R + gamma sum T V(s'): matches our delta_t(s) = V^_t(s) - val Q[V^]_t(s) with val = the stage matrix value. The definitions are [agent-read, rendered p.3]: V(s) = max_{pi in PD(A)} min_{o in O} sum_a Q(s,a,o) pi_a and Q(s,a,o) = R(s,a,o) + gamma sum_{s'} T(s,a,o,s') V(s'); for Markov games he says value iteration "can be shown to converge to the correct values [Owen, 1982]". Sec. 3 also says: "It is possible to define a notion of undiscounted rewards [Schwartz, 1993], but not all Markov games have optimal strategies in the undiscounted case [Owen, 1982]" (relevant to our finite-horizon/terminating choice; ours has terminal states, so it is not the case Littman excludes). Discounted, infinite horizon; the paper contains no convergence proof for minimax-Q (it cites Watkins-Dayan for single-agent Q-learning; convergence of minimax-Q is Littman-Szepesvari 1996 [memory]).

## 9. Ganzfried and Sandholm 2015: safe opponent exploitation; prior art for L2

**Citation.** S. Ganzfried, T. Sandholm. "Safe Opponent Exploitation." ACM Transactions on Economics and Computation 3(2), Article 8, April 2015. DOI 10.1145/2716322 (the page says "A shorter early version appeared in EC'12"). [read-pdf text; `GS15.pdf`, 28 pp]

**Safety notion [read-pdf text, Sec. 4]**: "Definition 4.1. A safe strategy for a repeated game is a strategy that guarantees a worst-case payoff of at least v_i per period in expectation." They note "The set of safe strategies is the same as the set of minimax strategies in the full repeated game." Repeated (strategic-form and extensive-form) games with finite T; safe = **zero slack in expectation over the T-fold repeated game**. Key concept: "gifts" (opponent mistakes that let the agent deviate safely); Prop. 5.3 and later characterise safe strategies; algorithms: play an eps-safe best response where eps is funded by accumulated gifts (RWYWE, BEFFE, etc.). Abstract [read-pdf text]: prior work "[McCracken and Bowling 2004] ... guarantees safety in the limit as the number of iterations goes to infinity; however, the algorithms in that paper can be arbitrarily exploitable in the finitely repeated game setting".

**Their statement of exactly our L2 construction [read-pdf text, Sec. 6.4 "Regret Minimization between an Equilibrium and an Opponent Exploitation Algorithm"]**: "We could use a no-regret algorithm (e.g., Exp3 [Auer et al. 2002]) to select between an equilibrium and an (unsafe) opponent exploitation algorithm at each iteration. As prior work has pointed out [McCracken and Bowling 2004], this would be safe in the limit as T -> inf. However, this would not be safe in finitely repeated games. Even in the infinitely repeated case, no-regret algorithms only guarantee that average regret goes to 0 in the limit; in fact, total regret can still grow arbitrarily large." Section 6.5: "Regret minimization in the space of equilibria is safe, but again would potentially miss out on a lot of exploitation... previously used... in Kuhn poker [Hoehn et al. 2005]." Intro: algorithms with eps-safety against specific opponent classes [Powers, Shoham, Vu 2007]; "by contrast, our algorithms achieve full safety against all opponents."

Further GS15 facts [agent-read, full read]: the proof of Prop. 6.4 (p.13) shows sum_{t=1}^T u_i(pi_i^t, pi_{-i}^t) = E[k^{T+1}] + T v* >= T v*, so "safe" means cumulative expected payoff >= T v* with zero slack; footnote 4 (p.14): "We recently discovered a mistake in [McCracken-Bowling's] proof of safety in the limit; however, the result is still correct" (erratum at http://webdocs.cs.ualberta.ca/~bowling/papers/04aaai-fallsymp-errata.pdf); on SPS (p.14): "even in infinitely repeated games, SPS can lose a significant amount; it is merely the average loss that approaches zero". Scope: "stochastic game", "Markov", "discount" do not occur in the paper; chance appears only as a game-tree player. The paper has no numbered Theorem; the characterisation "safe non-equilibrium strategies exist iff a gift strategy exists" is Proposition 5.3.

**How L2 differs.**
1. GS15 safety = exact (zero-slack) guarantee per period in expectation over the T-fold game; L2 gives **eps_T-safety with an explicit finite-sample slack R(T) + 2 eps_v E[T]** (sublinear in T, hence vanishing per game). GS15 reject this route as "not safe in finitely repeated games"; our claim is the weaker, quantified notion. Say so explicitly.
2. GS15's setting is repeated normal-form / extensive-form games with imperfect information; no stochastic games and no leaf error. L2 uses L1 to turn per-turn regret into a per-game loss to the value in a stochastic game with simultaneous moves, a heuristic leaf and an adaptive opponent. (Scope: GS15 treat "repeated" games and games with imperfect information, not stochastic games [read-pdf text]; I did not find a stochastic-game treatment.)
3. GS15 do not give the Hedge regret bound; they dismiss the approach. The matrix-game core of L2 (regret to the minimax expert implies payoff >= value - regret) is the folklore consequence of Hannan consistency and the minimax theorem: e.g., Lis13 Lemma 4.6 and KL18's intro [read-pdf text] ("If both players use a Hannan consistent algorithm... converge to a NE (Blum and Mansour 2007)"; one-sided version: G_max(t) >= t v for any opponent sequence).

**Relation.** Both RL best-response exploiters of strong agents are not model-free black-box RL: they use gray-box access to the victim inside search ("victim-play", A-MCTS / ISMCTS-BR). This supports the note's contrast "white-box planning vs undirected RL exploiter" and means a PPO exploiter with no search and no victim queries is a weaker comparison than ABR/A-MCTS. Wang23 and Tim20 are RL-with-search, so L3c's undirected-exploration proposition applies only to the model-free PPO exploiter, as the note already says in "Scope".

## 12. Error propagation for ADP: Munos, Scherrer, the gamma factor (Remark L5.5)

- **Munos 2007** (SIAM J. Control Optim. 46(2):541-561, DOI 10.1137/040614384; key Mun07): publisher and HAL copies were blocked; **not read**. Via AMPI [read-pdf text, p.6 Remark 1]: "existing point-wise error bounds for AVI (Munos, 2007, Lemma 4.1) and API (Munos, 2003, Corollary 10)... do not consider error in the greedy step (i.e., eps'_k = 0) and... have the following form: limsup_{k->inf} l_k <= 2 limsup sum_{i=1}^{k-1} sum_{j=i}^inf Gamma^j |eps_{k-i}|."
- **Scherrer, Ghavamzadeh, Gabillon, Geist, "Approximate Modified Policy Iteration," ICML 2012 (arXiv 1205.3054v2; the journal version is Scherrer, Ghavamzadeh, Gabillon, Lesner, Geist, JMLR 16, 2015 (pages from memory: 1629-1676), "...and its Application to the Game of Tetris"; key Sch15; the DOI shown by Undermind, 10.5555/2789272.2886802, is an ACM DL id, not a DOI)**. The PDF I read is the **ICML 2012 arXiv version, not JMLR**. **Theorem 1 [read-pdf text, p.6]**: for AMPI, ||l_k||_{p,rho} <= 2(gamma - gamma^k)(C_q^{1,k,0})^{1/p}/(1-gamma)^2 sup_{j<=k-1} ||eps_j||_{pq',mu} + (1-gamma^k)(C_q^{0,k,0})^{1/p}/(1-gamma)^2 sup_{j<=k} ||eps'_j||_{pq',mu} + g(k), with concentrability coefficients c_q(j) = max_{pi_1..pi_j} ||d(rho P_{pi_1}...P_{pi_j})/d mu||_{q,mu}. **Remark 2 / Eq. 17 (L_inf) [agent-read, rendered]**: ||l_k||_inf <= 2(gamma - gamma^k)/(1-gamma)^2 sup||eps_j||_inf + (1-gamma^k)/(1-gamma)^2 sup||eps'_j||_inf + 2 gamma^k/(1-gamma) min(||d_0||_inf, ||b_0||_inf); as k -> inf, limsup ||l_k||_inf <= (2 gamma sup||eps_j|| + sup||eps'_j||)/(1-gamma)^2, "a generalization of the API (m = inf) bound of Bertsekas and Tsitsiklis (1996, Prop. 6.2)". Setting: discounted MDP; "finite horizon" in the paper means the iteration count k, there is no statement about the effective horizon 1/(1-gamma) [agent-read].
- **Per15 Thm 1** (item 7c) is the same bound for zero-sum stochastic games (two-player, min-max operator).
**Relation to Remark L5.5.** The gamma-dependence in these bounds is two factors of 1/(1-gamma): one from summing errors along the discounted occupancy (sum_j gamma^j), one from the loss-vs-value-error conversion; finite-horizon undiscounted analogues replace sum_j gamma^j by the horizon (H or H-bar). Our L5.5 sentence is consistent with this; cite AMPI Thm 1 and Per15 Thm 1 for the exact discounted form and mark the "Munos 2007" cite as [memory] unless the PDF is obtained.
