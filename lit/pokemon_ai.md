## 2. Per-paper notes (quotes)

**Foul Play / poke-engine** (https://pmariglia.github.io/posts/foul-play ; https://github.com/pmariglia/foul-play ; https://github.com/pmariglia/poke-engine). Web fetch summaries, not full-text verified.
- "root-parallelized Monte Carlo Tree Search on many battle states", guided "by a custom evaluation function rather than rollouts".
- Old expectiminimax: "search beyond about 5 turns ahead was practically impossible without timing out"; flaw that opponent moved after us; fix: "MCTS is modified to use Decoupled Upper Confidence Bound applied to Trees (DUCT)".
- "Foul Play keeps a large list of possible sets for each opposing Pokemon and filters them down"; "In random formats, each Pokemon has a small number of possible sets, often fewer than a dozen"; "samples several possible battle states by filling in the unknown attributes" with likelihood weights. That is PIMC-style determinization, with no equilibrium.
- Ratings: gen9randombattle peak 2341 (Top 50); gen9ou 1879. Blog says GXE/Glicko are better than raw Elo.
- poke-engine README: documents expectiminimax, iterative-deepening, MCTS, calculate-damage; prints a `matrix:` and iterative-deepening output contains `NaN`; README does not explain NaN, does not mention Nash, and says nothing about root alpha-beta pruning beyond an `--ab-prune` flag. So our NaN finding (55-80% pruned) is undocumented upstream (novel observation, but we should cite our own measurement).

**Ihara et al. 2018 (Iha18)** [reader]. "Most of the uncertainty of Pokemon occurs in the simultaneous move nodes" (Sec V). "we used EXP3 as the edge choice algorithm ... simultaneous move node of DMCTS" (Sec VI-B). PGL "top 10 probabilities of moves ... abilities, natures, and items" as prior (Sec IV). Table I: ISMCTS beats DMCTS 57.5% but only 26.5% vs CEMCTS and 25.0% vs CMCTS (perfect-information variants); average 36.3%. Table II: as opponent-team uncertainty shrinks (3-of-6 to 3-of-3), ISMCTS 36.3 to 40.7%, DMCTS 28.5 to 34.5%, perfect-info ones fall. Relevance: closest older precedent to our PIMC-vs-information-set comparison, and an oracle-gap-like result (perfect-info beats hidden-info searchers). Caveat: their perfect-info agents see the true opponent team.

**Sarantinos 2022 (Sar22b)** [reader]. Format is Gen7 Random Battle (not VGC despite background). "forward pruning algorithm to prune states because alpha beta pruning cannot be used in games like this where players decide simultaneously" (4.1.1). This matches our NaN-from-root-pruning failure: alpha-beta on simultaneous-move payoff matrices is unsound. "Our approach is based on a regret minimization Machine Learning algorithm" to predict opponent moves from history, then best response to the move with lowest payoff (4.1.2), i.e. maximin-flavored. Unknown opp species: MILP choosing representative mons; known species: most probable property values from usage statistics (argmax of prior, no belief posterior). Peak rank 33, 643 games, 4 x c5a.24xlarge.

**Wang 2024 (MIT M.Eng.)**, https://hdl.handle.net/1721.1/153888. Abstract (via DSpace): "The agent peaked at rank 8 (1693 Elo) on the official Pokemon Showdown gen4randombattles ladder"; MCTS guided by PPO actor-critic via self-play. Only abstract read; compute and details not verified. Note VGC-Bench's reference list mislabels it as a PhD thesis.

**PokeLLMon (Hu24e)** [reader]. Gen8 random battle; Table 7: 48.57% vs ladder (105), 56.00% vs invited player (50). Vs heuristic bot 26% (GPT-4 base, Table 1) up to 64% with self-consistency (Table 5). Paper notes it fails to predict the opponent's next action (Sec 7.2): "makes decision only based on the current state information". No belief model.

**PokeChamp (Kar25b)** [reader]. "we focus on pure strategies and compute the best action that is safe even against the adversarial play of the opponent". Maximin over sampled action pairs. Gen9 OU Table 2: PokeChamp-GPT-4o 84%, Elo 1268 vs Abyssal 1117; vs PokeLLMon 76% (Fig 7). Gen8 randbats (Table 4): 70% no Dynamax, 56% with (Table 5). Ladder Elo est. 1300-1500; about one third of games lost to timeouts. Hidden stats: "historical data to estimate the likelihood of different stat distributions" plus LLM predictions, not formal Bayes.

**Metamon (Gri25)** [reader]. "we are relying entirely on memory to infer the opponent's team". Data: ">475k human demonstrations ... about 950k sequences containing 38M timesteps" (Sec 3); models 15M/50M/200M; "8x NVIDIA A5000 ... at least 1M gradient steps" (App E.4). Vs Foul Play (Sec 5.4, Fig 11a, 300 battles/gen): "best version of the bot to a draw in Gens 3 and 4 ... outperform it ... Gens 1 and 2". Ladder: SynRL-V2 "inside the top decile". Chose Gen1-4 because no team preview; explicitly avoids random battles ("more casual player base"). Training on realistic self-play data "can produce inconsistent improvement"; self-play there is data generation + offline training. Relevance: compute scale of RL (millions of trajectories, multi-GPU) vs our 30M-decision/25-min PPO that plateaued at 6% vs MCTS.

**VGC-Bench (Ang25b)** [reader]. PPO under SP/FP/DO; 8 x A40; 5,013,504 timesteps (Table 7). "non-BC methods achieve decent performance, [but] they are generally outperformed by methods initialized with BC". Exploitability: "in almost all cases, all agents are approximately 100% exploitable". DO uses LP Nash over policy pool. Supports "pure self-play RL is weak without imitation/search".

**PokeAgent Challenge (Kar26c)** [reader]. Battles are "two-player, zero-sum, stochastic games with imperfect information and simultaneous action selection" (3.1). Gen9 OU champion FoulPlay (DUCT); final 50-14. Claims FoulPlay Gen9 randbats ">90% GXE", peak 2341. Metamon Gen9 OU est. GXE 71% (Kakuna). Top entries are not equilibrium solvers. Appendix E.1.2: search depth 10+ turns on promising lines vs ~5 for exhaustive expectiminimax. Confirms MCTS (plus engine) is state-of-the-art in Gen9 OU/randbats.

**PokaiTrainer (Yu26m/Yu26n)**, arXiv:2608.29197, VGC doubles with Open Team Sheets. Most relevant. Spot-checked in PDF text.
- "CFR-based subgame solving over public belief states with value-network leaves". "We solve every decision the agent faces ... as a Bayesian matrix game." Belief reweighted by engine likelihood: "a candidate is reweighted by the mass the engine's own transition puts on the observed public outcome and move order" (4.4).
- Argmax vs sampling: eval cells play argmax of average strategy; ladder samples at T=0.5; text says fixed eval opponents do not adapt. Table 9 (App E.5, spot-checked): informed best response wins 77.7% [72.2, 82.4] vs CFR argmax, 52.3% [46.2, 58.4] vs T=0.5, 49.6% [43.5, 55.7] vs full mix; decoupled UCT: 74.6 / 72.7 / 83.6%.
- Decoupled UCT vs CFR agent 51.7% (2,048 games) head-to-head, yet exploitable (83.6% for informed BR). So mixing buys robustness against adaptive adversaries; against fixed opponents argmax is used.
- Compute: ~250 L40S-hours self-play + 15 A100-hours training; median 2.7 s/decision (native), 23 s scaled. Ladder 89-61 sets (59.3%), peak 1492, representative 1350-1400, GXE 72-74.
- No PIMC/determinization baseline (reader: "reports no direct comparison"), and hidden spreads of different mons assumed independent (App A.2). Note: two Undermind records (Yu26m "Equilibrium Search", Yu26n "Belief-State Search") are versions of the same arXiv id.

**Yu, Olshevsky, Chin 2024 (Yu24c)** [reader]. NN-CCE; not evaluated on Pokemon; samples from the CCE policy. Mentions equilibrium-mismatch with non-equilibrium opponents (App 8.1.3). Peripheral; cite only for simultaneous-move search theory.

## 4. Gaps our project fills
- No paper solves a per-turn equilibrium of the *unpruned* joint-action matrix in Gen 9 randbats and benchmarks against engine MCTS; Foul Play uses DUCT, PokeChamp maximin on sampled pairs.
- Direct agent-form Bayesian matrix game (LP) vs PIMC with actual randbats role-set priors and an oracle upper bound; Yu26m has belief-state CFR in VGC without PIMC baseline.
- Quantified blend-loss bound (4*delta_H) validated on 20k real decisions.
- Argmax vs sampling of equilibrium against non-adaptive opponents measured directly.
- Caution on what we cannot claim: ladder Elo (not measured), strength vs humans, exploitability against an adaptive best response (where Yu26m shows mixing matters).

## 7. Cross-reference of RESULTS.md conclusions

Keys below match section 6 BibTeX: yu2026pokaitrainer, karten2025pokechamp, grigsby2025metamon, hu2024pokellmon, karten2026pokeagent, angliss2025vgcbench, ihara2018ismcts, sarantinos2022teamwork, wang2024randbats, yu2024nncce, mariglia2024foulplay. Support/contradict labels rest on the reader-extracted facts in section 2 (not all spot-checked). "Prior art" flags mark things we must not present as new. Generic PIMC/strategy-fusion theory (Frank-Basin, Long et al., Cowling) is outside this Pokemon search; see game_theory_noregret.md.

### Headline items

**H1 / C1. Agent-form Bayesian solver beats PIMC (0.585 [0.540, 0.630], n=400).**
- Support (directional): ihara2018ismcts, ISMCTS beats determinized DMCTS 57.5% (Table I, 200 games). Foul Play (mariglia2024foulplay) is itself sampled-state/determinization search, so it is the PIMC-style comparison class.
- Prior art: a Bayesian matrix game with per-world payoff matrices and belief weights is the core of yu2026pokaitrainer (Sec 4.2 "We solve every decision ... as a Bayesian matrix game"; one regret table per opponent-information world). Our novelty is NOT the Bayesian-game formulation. It is (i) the agent-form LP over 4 sampled worlds at the per-turn level in Gen 9 randbats, (ii) the head-to-head against PIMC blending at equal compute, which yu2026pokaitrainer did not run (reader: "no direct comparison with PIMC or a determinization-based search baseline").
- Differences to state: ihara2018ismcts uses MCTS/EXP3 info-sets, not LP; yu2026pokaitrainer is VGC with open team sheets and CFR plus value net.
- Citation text: "Per-turn Bayesian matrix games over opponent-information worlds were used in PokaiTrainer's belief-state CFR [yu2026pokaitrainer]; we isolate the effect of solving the worlds jointly (agent-form LP) versus averaging per-world equilibria (PIMC) at equal compute in Gen 9 Random Battles, and find a 0.585 [0.540, 0.630] score for the joint solve."

**H2 / C2. Blend-loss bound 4*delta_H holds on 46,890 decisions; use as gating diagnostic.**
- Not addressed by any Pokemon paper located. yu2026pokaitrainer notes only that cross-mon independence of hidden spreads is a simplification whose cost was "not measured" (App A.2). Novel as far as this search shows; the theory is game-theoretic, check game_theory_noregret.md before claiming novelty of the bound itself.
- Citation text: "To our knowledge no prior Pokemon agent quantifies the loss from averaging per-world equilibria; we bound it by 4*delta_H and verify the bound on 46,890 real decisions."

**H3 / C4. Full-info matrix solving beats MCTS only with 25x more time (0.58 top move, 0.56 safest vs MCTS-2000; 0.345 / 0.263 vs time-matched MCTS at 75 ms, X1); sampling 0.44; randomizing only costs vs non-adaptive opponent.**
- Support (reasoning): yu2026pokaitrainer plays argmax in evaluation because fixed opponents "do not adapt", and samples at T=0.5 only on the ladder; Table 9 (spot-checked): informed best response wins 77.7% vs argmax, 52.3% vs T=0.5, 49.6% vs full mix. So mixing buys robustness against an adaptive adversary, which matches our C4 interpretation and our "E6 not yet measured" caveat.
- Prior art / do not overclaim: that argmax is adequate against fixed opponents and mixing is needed against adaptive ones is already argued in yu2026pokaitrainer. Also "deterministic play is exploitable" is stated there (Sec 2.1). Our contribution is the direct measurement of the cost of sampling against a non-adaptive engine bot (0.58 vs 0.44), which yu2026pokaitrainer says it did not isolate. yu2024nncce hints at equilibrium-mismatch with non-equilibrium opponents (App 8.1.3) but is not Pokemon.
- Prior art on maximin: karten2025pokechamp already plays a maximin pure strategy ("safe even against the adversarial play of the opponent") and sarantinos2022teamwork a best response to the lowest-payoff opponent move. Our "safest move" is in that family, not new.
- MCTS vs matrix solver: karten2026pokeagent shows FoulPlay (DUCT) as Gen 9 OU champion; yu2026pokaitrainer finds decoupled UCT at parity (51.7%, 2,048 games) with CFR agent in VGC. UPDATE (X1, 2026-11-14): the 0.58 vs poke-engine MCTS-2000 was not compute-matched (about 70 vs 2.7 ms); at matched time (MCTS 75 ms/move) top-move play scores 0.345 [0.289, 0.401] and safest 0.263 [0.210, 0.315]. This agrees with the literature: MCTS/DUCT is the strong baseline (karten2026pokeagent), and PokaiTrainer only reaches parity with decoupled UCT by adding a learned value net to CFR. Do not claim matrix solving beats MCTS.
- Citation text: "Against a non-adaptive MCTS opponent at 2,000 iterations (25x less time per move; at matched time MCTS wins), playing the top equilibrium action scores 0.58 while sampling the equilibrium mix scores 0.44; this is consistent with the argument in [yu2026pokaitrainer] that argmax suffices against fixed opponents and mixing is for robustness to adaptive best responses (which we leave to future work)."

**H4 / C6. poke-engine iterative-deepening matrix is 55-80% pruned; pruned matrices are not payoff matrices.**
- Support: sarantinos2022teamwork states "alpha beta pruning cannot be used in games like this where players decide simultaneously". The poke-engine README (mariglia2024foulplay repos) documents `matrix:` output with NaN entries but does not explain them or mention Nash. So the engine behaviour is visible upstream but undocumented.
- Prior art: the principle (alpha-beta is invalid for simultaneous moves) is known (sarantinos2022teamwork). Our new piece is the quantification and the demonstration that it silently breaks equilibrium methods (0.434 vs 0.561 safest; 0.302 vs 0.443 sampled).
- Citation text: "Alpha-beta pruning is unsound for simultaneous-move payoff matrices [sarantinos2022teamwork]; in poke-engine's iterative-deepening search 55-80% of root cells are left unevaluated (NaN), and using them as a payoff matrix reduces our safest-move bot from 0.561 to 0.434 against MCTS."

**H5 / C5-adjacent. Plain PPO self-play: 30M decisions, 25 min, 5-13% vs MCTS-200 in a 12-species full-information setting.**
- Support: grigsby2025metamon needs offline RL on 475k human battles (950k traj, 38M timesteps), 15M-200M parameter Transformers, 8 x A5000, >=1M gradient steps; angliss2025vgcbench: PPO self-play on 8 x A40, 5.0M timesteps, BC init outperforms non-BC, agents "approximately 100% exploitable"; yu2026pokaitrainer: about 250 L40S-hours, and a round-1 network under search beats the PPO policy 73-27 (search is central); wang2024randbats reaches rank 8 gen4 randbats only with MCTS guided by PPO (abstract only). None reports a laptop-scale plain PPO result, so we neither contradict nor duplicate; ours is a cost reference only (as RESULTS.md says).
- Citation text: "Consistent with prior work that relies on large offline datasets, imitation initialization, or search on top of the learned policy [grigsby2025metamon, angliss2025vgcbench, yu2026pokaitrainer, wang2024randbats], plain PPO self-play at laptop scale (30M decisions, 25 min) wins only 5-13% against MCTS."

### Numbered conclusions not covered above

**C3. RM+ with 100 iterations matches exact LP; the bottleneck is the matrix, not the solver.**
- Support/related: yu2026pokaitrainer uses linear CFR with alternating updates (approximate equilibrium, no LP) and still plays strongly, so approximate no-regret solving suffices there too; angliss2025vgcbench uses LP for double oracle (exact, small matrix). sarantinos2022teamwork also uses regret minimization over the payoff matrix for opponent modelling. No paper compares RM+ and LP head-to-head in play. Check game_theory_noregret.md for RM+ literature (Tammelin) which is outside this search. Claim is a small empirical confirmation, not novel in principle.
- Citation text: "As in PokaiTrainer's linear-CFR solve [yu2026pokaitrainer], approximate no-regret solving is sufficient per turn: RM+ at 100 iterations is indistinguishable from the exact LP in play (0.501 vs 0.504)."

**C5. Information is valuable: oracle beats agent-form by about 0.1; better beliefs are the lever.**
- Support: ihara2018ismcts, perfect-information MCTS variants beat the hidden-information ISMCTS (25.0-26.5% for ISMCTS vs CMCTS/CEMCTS, Table I) and the gap narrows as team uncertainty shrinks (Table II, 36.3 to 40.7% for "3 from 6" to "3 from 3"). Opposite design choice: grigsby2025metamon infers the opponent implicitly via history; no explicit oracle gap reported. mariglia2024foulplay and karten2026pokeagent describe evidence-based set filtering (damage constrains stats, speed, items), supporting item/ability/behaviour inference as the lever we propose; this is prior art in practice (Foul Play) so do not call item/speed inference novel. yu2026pokaitrainer reweights stat-spread candidates from engine outcome likelihoods.
- Not contradicted. Caveat: ihara2018ismcts perfect-info agents see the true opponent team, similar to our oracle, but the metric differs (pairwise win rates between algorithms).
- Citation text: "Perfect-information search outperforms information-set search in Pokemon [ihara2018ismcts]; our oracle (true hidden sets) beats the Bayesian agent-form solver by about 0.1, quantifying the headroom for better set/item inference such as that used by Foul Play [mariglia2024foulplay]."
