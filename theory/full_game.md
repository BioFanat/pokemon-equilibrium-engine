# Full-game exploitability of the per-turn matrix-game agent

Draft 2026-11-14. Companion to `full_game_targets.md` (targets), `../notes/math_framing.md` (L1–L6), `../lit/game_theory_noregret.md` (cite keys), `../engine/RESULTS.md` (numbers). Code: `../engine/nash.py` (`full_matrix`, `rm_plus`), `../engine/hidden_agents.py` (`agent_form_lp`).

Conventions. $\Delta$ denotes the **payoff range** of a stage matrix (max entry $-$ min entry); for terminal payoffs in $[-1,1]$, $\Delta\le 2$. `math_framing.md` writes $R$ for this range; the targets file writes "payoffs in $[-R,R]$", which is a range of $2R$ — the two conventions differ by a factor $2$ in every belief term (see Theorem B, Remark 2). $\mathrm{TV}(b,\hat b)=\tfrac12\|b-\hat b\|_1$. $\Delta(X)$ is the probability simplex on a finite set $X$ (context separates it from the range $\Delta$). For a matrix $A\in\mathbb R^{n\times m}$, $\|A\|_\infty=\max_{a,\beta}|A_{a\beta}|$ and $\operatorname{val}(A)=\max_{x\in\Delta([n])}\min_{y\in\Delta([m])}x^\top A y$ (maximin; the inner minimum is attained at a pure column, so it is a maximum of a continuous function on a compact set). Agent = row player = maximizer.

Everything in §§2–5 and the proved part of §6 is elementary and self-contained except for four standard results used as black boxes: existence of the value and of optimal behavioural strategies in finite zero-sum games with perfect recall (von Neumann; Kuhn 1953), Sion's minimax theorem (Sion 1958), Hoeffding's inequality and the sub-Gaussian maximal inequality (e.g. Boucheron, Lugosi & Massart 2013, *Concentration Inequalities*, OUP, Thm 2.8 and §2.5). None of these four is in the lit review — **not in lit review, check**.

**Verification.** Lean 4 (Mathlib) proofs in `theory/lean/FullGame/Basic.lean` cover Lemma 1 (`lemma1_one_step`), Lemma 2(a) (`val_lipschitz`, `exists_val`), Theorem A's backward induction in abstract form (`backward_induction`, `deficit_le_sum`, `theoremA_abstract`) and the RM+ step (`rmplus_improvement`); no `sorry`, and the axioms are propext / Classical.choice / Quot.sound only. ## 1. Model and definitions (full information)

**Game $G$.** Finite state set $S$, horizon $H$, stages $t=0,\dots,H-1$. A subset $Z\subset S$ of terminal states, absorbing, with payoff $u:Z\to[-1,1]$. At a non-terminal $s$ at stage $t<H$ the agent picks $a\in A(s)$ and the opponent $\beta\in B(s)$ simultaneously; the next state is drawn from $P(\cdot\mid s,a,\beta)$ (chance). If the game is still live at stage $H$ the payoff is $u_H(s)\in[-1,1]$ (engine: $u_H\equiv 0$, a draw). Write $u(s_\tau)$ for the realised payoff, where $\tau=\min\{t\le H: s_t\in Z\text{ or }t=H\}$ is the length.

**Values.** $V^*_H(s)=u(s)$ for $s\in Z$, $u_H(s)$ otherwise; for $t<H$: $V^*_t(s)=u(s)$ if $s\in Z$, else
$$Q^*_t(s)(a,\beta)=\sum_{s'}P(s'\mid s,a,\beta)\,V^*_{t+1}(s'),\qquad V^*_t(s)=\operatorname{val}\big(Q^*_t(s)\big).$$

**Strategies.** A history is $h_t=(s_0,a_0,\beta_0,s_1,\dots,s_t)$. An opponent strategy $\sigma$ assigns to every history a mixed stage action $\sigma(h_t)\in\Delta(B(s_t))$ — it may condition on everything, including the agent's past realised actions. (Opponent strategies that randomise over whole plans are averages of such $\sigma$, and all bounds below are linear in that average, so they are covered.) The agent $\pi$ is **Markov**: $x_t(s)\in\Delta(A(s))$ depends only on $(t,s)$. Given $(\pi,\sigma)$ the expected payoff is $W_0(s_0)$, where $W$ is defined on the finite history tree by $W_t(h_t)=u(s_t)$ if $s_t\in Z$, $W_H(h_H)=u_H(s_H)$, and otherwise
$$W_t(h_t)=\sum_{a,\beta,s'}x_t(s_t)(a)\,\sigma(h_t)(\beta)\,P(s'\mid s_t,a,\beta)\,W_{t+1}(h_t a\beta s').\tag{1.1}$$
Because moves are simultaneous, $a_t$ and $\beta_t$ are drawn independently given $h_t$; this is what (1.1) encodes.

**Agent.** At each live $(t,s)$ the agent builds $\hat Q_t(s)$ with $\|\hat Q_t(s)-Q^*_t(s)\|_\infty\le\varepsilon_v(t,s)$ and plays $x_t(s)$ that is $\varepsilon_s(t,s)$-optimal in $\hat Q_t(s)$: $\min_y x_t(s)^\top\hat Q_t(s)\,y\ge\operatorname{val}(\hat Q_t(s))-\varepsilon_s(t,s)$. Put $\delta(t,s)=\varepsilon_s(t,s)+2\varepsilon_v(t,s)$ for live $s$ and $\delta(t,s)=0$ for $s\in Z$; $\varepsilon_s=\sup\varepsilon_s(t,s)$, $\varepsilon_v=\sup\varepsilon_v(t,s)$.

**Exploitability.** $\operatorname{expl}(\pi)=V^*_0(s_0)-\inf_\sigma W_0^{\pi,\sigma}(s_0)$.

**Proposition 0 (Shapley 1953; self-contained here).** $V^*_0(s_0)$ is the value of $G$: the agent playing an exact stage maximin strategy of $Q^*_t(s)$ at every $(t,s)$ guarantees $\ge V^*_0(s_0)$ against every $\sigma$ (Theorem A with $\delta\equiv 0$), and the opponent playing an exact stage minimax strategy of $Q^*_t(s)$ at every $(t,s)$ holds every agent to $\le V^*_0(s_0)$ (the mirror-image induction). Hence $\operatorname{expl}(\pi)\ge 0$ and it is the standard exploitability.

## 2. One-step lemmas

**Lemma 1 (one step).** Let $A,B\in\mathbb R^{n\times m}$ with $\|A-B\|_\infty\le\varepsilon$ and let $x\in\Delta([n])$ be $\varepsilon_s$-optimal in $B$. Then $\min_y x^\top A y\ge\operatorname{val}(A)-\varepsilon_s-2\varepsilon$.

*Proof.* (i) For all $x\in\Delta([n]),y\in\Delta([m])$: $|x^\top Ay-x^\top By|=|\sum_{a,\beta}x_a y_\beta(A-B)_{a\beta}|\le\sum_{a,\beta}x_ay_\beta\,\varepsilon=\varepsilon$. (ii) Let $x_A$ attain $\operatorname{val}(A)$. By (i), $\min_y x_A^\top By\ge\min_y x_A^\top Ay-\varepsilon=\operatorname{val}(A)-\varepsilon$, so $\operatorname{val}(B)\ge\operatorname{val}(A)-\varepsilon$. (iii) By (i) again, $\min_y x^\top Ay\ge\min_y x^\top By-\varepsilon\ge\operatorname{val}(B)-\varepsilon_s-\varepsilon\ge\operatorname{val}(A)-\varepsilon_s-2\varepsilon$. $\square$

*Tightness of the factor 2.* $n=2,m=1$: $A=(0,-2\varepsilon)^\top$, $B=(-\varepsilon,-\varepsilon)^\top$; $\|A-B\|_\infty=\varepsilon$, $x=e_2$ is $0$-optimal in $B$, and $x^\top A=-2\varepsilon=\operatorname{val}(A)-2\varepsilon$.

**Lemma 2 (Lipschitz facts).**
(a) $|\operatorname{val}(A)-\operatorname{val}(B)|\le\|A-B\|_\infty$. *Proof:* step (ii) above and its mirror image.
(b) Chance averaging is non-expansive: if $(\mathcal P V)(a,\beta)=\sum_{s'}P(s'\mid s,a,\beta)V(s')$ then $\|\mathcal PV-\mathcal PV'\|_\infty\le\|V-V'\|_\infty$. *Proof:* a convex combination of numbers each within $\|V-V'\|_\infty$.
(c) (Lookahead.) Let the agent use exact depth-$d$ lookahead with a leaf estimate $\hat V$ at stage $t+d$, $\|\hat V-V^*_{t+d}\|_\infty\le\varepsilon_{\mathrm{leaf}}$: $\hat V^{(0)}=\hat V$, $\hat Q^{(k)}=\mathcal P\hat V^{(k-1)}$ (exact transition model), $\hat V^{(k)}(s)=\operatorname{val}(\hat Q^{(k)}(s))$ for live $s$ and $u(s)$ for terminal $s$, and $\hat Q_t=\hat Q^{(d)}$. Then $\varepsilon_v\le\varepsilon_{\mathrm{leaf}}$ for every $d\ge 1$. *Proof:* induction on $k$ using (b) then (a): $\|\hat Q^{(k)}-Q^*_{t+d-k}\|_\infty\le\|\hat V^{(k-1)}-V^*_{t+d-k+1}\|_\infty\le\varepsilon_{\mathrm{leaf}}$.
(d) (No contraction.) The bound in (c) is attained for every $d$ in games where no transition before stage $t+d$ reaches a terminal state: take $\hat V=V^*_{t+d}+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{leaf}}$ on live states; then $\hat Q^{(k)}=Q^*+\varepsilon_{\mathrm{leaf}}$ exactly at every level (a constant shift passes through averaging and through $\operatorname{val}$). With a discount $\gamma<1$ the same induction would give $\gamma^d\varepsilon_{\mathrm{leaf}}$; without discounting there is nothing to gain from depth in the worst case. (A constant shift does not change the agent's strategy; a non-constant leaf error of size $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{leaf}}$ sitting under decision-free chains flips a root decision at any depth, so the strategy-relevant error is also not reduced by depth in the worst case.)

In `nash.py:full_matrix` the stage matrix is one exact chance-averaged step over the engine's branches with a depth-1 heuristic leaf (`_leaf`, midpoint of the two sides' maximin values), so (c) with $d=1$ applies with $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{leaf}}=\sup_{s'}|\hat V(s')-V^*_{t+1}(s')|$ — after the heuristic units are mapped to the $[-1,1]$ scale; that mapping error is part of $\varepsilon_{\mathrm{leaf}}$ and is not measured.

## 3. Theorem A (full information)

**Theorem A.** For every history-dependent opponent $\sigma$,
$$W_0^{\pi,\sigma}(s_0)\;\ge\;V^*_0(s_0)-\mathbb E_{\pi,\sigma}\Big[\sum_{t<\tau}\delta(t,s_t)\Big].\tag{A.0}$$
Consequently
$$\operatorname{expl}(\pi)\le\sup_\sigma\mathbb E_{\pi,\sigma}\Big[\sum_{t<\tau}\delta(t,s_t)\Big]\le\sum_{t<H}\sup_{s\notin Z}\delta(t,s)\le H(\varepsilon_s+2\varepsilon_v).\tag{A.1}$$

*Proof.* Fix $\sigma$. For a history $h_t$ let $D_t(h_t)=\mathbb E_{\pi,\sigma}[\sum_{t\le t'<\tau}\delta(t',s_{t'})\mid h_t]$, defined on the tree by $D_t(h_t)=0$ if $s_t\in Z$ or $t=H$, and otherwise $D_t(h_t)=\delta(t,s_t)+\sum_{a,\beta,s'}x_t(s_t)(a)\sigma(h_t)(\beta)P(s'\mid s_t,a,\beta)D_{t+1}(h_ta\beta s')$. We show $W_t(h_t)\ge V^*_t(s_t)-D_t(h_t)$ for all $h_t$ by backward induction on $t$.

Base: $t=H$, or $s_t\in Z$: $W_t=V^*_t=u$ and $D_t=0$. Step: let $s=s_t\notin Z$, $x=x_t(s)$, $y=\sigma(h_t)\in\Delta(B(s))$. By the induction hypothesis applied to each child history,
$$W_t(h_t)\ge\sum_{a,\beta,s'}x(a)y(\beta)P(s'\mid s,a,\beta)\big[V^*_{t+1}(s')-D_{t+1}(h_ta\beta s')\big]=x^\top Q^*_t(s)\,y-\big(D_t(h_t)-\delta(t,s)\big).$$
By Lemma 1 with $A=Q^*_t(s)$, $B=\hat Q_t(s)$: $x^\top Q^*_t(s)y\ge\min_{y'}x^\top Q^*_t(s)y'\ge\operatorname{val}(Q^*_t(s))-\delta(t,s)=V^*_t(s)-\delta(t,s)$. Combining gives $W_t(h_t)\ge V^*_t(s)-D_t(h_t)$. At the root, (A.0). Taking $\inf_\sigma$ gives the first inequality of (A.1); the second follows from $\delta(t,s_t)\le\sup_{s\notin Z}\delta(t,s)$ and $\tau\le H$; the third from the definition of $\varepsilon_s,\varepsilon_v$. $\square$

The opponent's ability to condition on the agent's past realised actions is harmless because the induction is over histories: at each $h_t$ the agent's stage mixture is fixed and the opponent's reply $\sigma(h_t)$ is just some $y$, against which Lemma 1 holds uniformly. **A1** is the middle inequality of (A.1) (sup over live states, summed over stages).

**A2 (expected length).** Let $\bar\delta=\sup_{t,s\notin Z}\delta(t,s)$. Since (A.0) holds for *every* $\sigma$, it holds for any best response $\sigma^*\in\arg\min_\sigma W_0^{\pi,\sigma}(s_0)$, giving the correct "expected length" statement
$$\operatorname{expl}(\pi)=V^*_0(s_0)-W_0^{\pi,\sigma^*}(s_0)\le\mathbb E_{\pi,\sigma^*}\Big[\sum_{t<\tau}\delta(t,s_t)\Big]\le\bar\delta\;\mathbb E_{\pi,\sigma^*}[\tau].\tag{A.2}$$
So $H$ can be replaced by the expected length **under a best response to $\pi$** (the length of the games the exploiter actually plays), and more finely the per-stage error is charged only on live turns at the realised states. It **cannot** be replaced by the expected length under *an* equilibrium opponent (equilibrium opponents need not be unique: here "end at once" and "cont always" are both minimax-optimal, with lengths $1$ and $H$), under self-play, or under the opponents one usually meets:

*Counterexample.* $H\delta\le 1$. States $s_c$ ($c=0,\dots,H$) and terminal $z_c$ with $u(z_c)=-c\delta$. $A(s_c)=\{g,b\}$, $B(s_c)=\{\mathrm{end},\mathrm{cont}\}$ for $t<H-1$ and $\{\mathrm{end}\}$ at $t=H-1$. Deterministic transitions: $(\cdot,\mathrm{end})\to z_{c+\mathbf 1[b]}$, $(\cdot,\mathrm{cont})\to s_{c+\mathbf 1[b]}$. Backward induction gives $V^*_t(s_c)=-c\delta$ (every column of $Q^*_t(s_c)$ is $(-c\delta,-(c+1)\delta)^\top$), so $V^*_0(s_0)=0$. Agent $\pi$: $\hat Q=Q^*$ ($\varepsilon_v=0$) and $x=e_b$, which is $\delta$-optimal: $\varepsilon_s(t,s_c)=\delta$. The opponent "end at once" is minimax-optimal (it holds every agent to $-\delta\mathbf 1[b]\le 0=V^*_0$) and gives $\tau=1$, so "$\bar\delta\times$ length under an optimal opponent" is $\delta$. But $\sigma^*=$ "cont always" yields payoff $-H\delta$, so $\operatorname{expl}(\pi)=H\delta$, which is (A.2) with equality and exceeds the naive bound by the factor $H$.

## 4. Corollary A′ (rate in RM+ iterations)

Setting: a stage matrix $M\in\mathbb R^{n\times m}$ with range $\Delta$, $u(x,y)=x^\top My$. Regret Matching$^+$ for the row player: $Q_0=0\in\mathbb R^n$; $x_t=Q_{t-1}/\|Q_{t-1}\|_1$ if $Q_{t-1}\ne 0$, else uniform; after observing the reward vector $g_t\in\mathbb R^n$ (any sequence, possibly chosen after seeing $x_t$) set $r_t=g_t-\langle x_t,g_t\rangle\mathbf 1$ and $Q_t=(Q_{t-1}+r_t)^+$.

**Lemma 3 (RM+ regret; Tammelin 2014 [Tam14]; Tammelin, Burch, Johanson & Bowling, IJCAI 2015 — not in lit review, check).** If every $g_t$ has range $\le\Delta$, then for every $a$ and every $1\le k\le T$: $\sum_{t=k}^T r_t(a)\le Q_T(a)\le\|Q_T\|_2\le\Delta\sqrt{nT}$. In particular the cumulative regret $R_T=\max_a\sum_{t\le T}r_t(a)\le\Delta\sqrt{nT}$, and the linearly weighted regret satisfies $\sum_{t\le T}t\,r_t(a)\le T\,Q_T(a)\le\Delta\sqrt n\,T^{3/2}$.

*Proof.* $Q_t\ge Q_{t-1}+r_t$ componentwise, so $Q_T\ge Q_{k-1}+\sum_{t=k}^Tr_t\ge\sum_{t=k}^Tr_t$ since $Q_{k-1}\ge 0$. Next, $|v^+_i|\le|v_i|$ gives $\|Q_t\|_2^2\le\|Q_{t-1}+r_t\|_2^2=\|Q_{t-1}\|_2^2+2\langle Q_{t-1},r_t\rangle+\|r_t\|_2^2$, and $\langle Q_{t-1},r_t\rangle=\langle Q_{t-1},g_t\rangle-\|Q_{t-1}\|_1\langle x_t,g_t\rangle=0$ by the choice of $x_t$ (trivially $0$ if $Q_{t-1}=0$). Since $|r_t(a)|=|g_t(a)-\langle x_t,g_t\rangle|\le\Delta$, $\|Q_T\|_2^2\le\sum_t\|r_t\|_2^2\le Tn\Delta^2$. Finally $\sum_t t\,r_t(a)=\sum_{k=1}^T\sum_{t=k}^Tr_t(a)\le T\,Q_T(a)$. $\square$

**Proposition 4 (simultaneous RM+, linear averaging).** Run RM+ for both players with simultaneous updates ($g^x_t=My_t$, $g^y_t=-M^\top x_t$) and output $\bar x=\sum_t t\,x_t/W$, $\bar y=\sum_t t\,y_t/W$, $W=T(T+1)/2$. Then
$$\max_a u(a,\bar y)-\min_\beta u(\bar x,\beta)\le\frac{2\Delta(\sqrt n+\sqrt m)}{\sqrt T},\qquad\text{hence}\qquad\varepsilon_s:=\operatorname{val}(M)-\min_\beta u(\bar x,\beta)\le\frac{2\Delta(\sqrt n+\sqrt m)}{\sqrt T}\le\frac{4\Delta\sqrt{\max(n,m)}}{\sqrt T}.$$
With uniform averaging the constant $2$ becomes $1$ (use $R_T$ instead of the weighted regret).

*Proof.* For every $a$: $\sum_t t\,[u(a,y_t)-u(x_t,y_t)]=\sum_t t\,r^x_t(a)\le\Delta\sqrt n\,T^{3/2}$; for every $\beta$: $\sum_t t\,[u(x_t,y_t)-u(x_t,\beta)]\le\Delta\sqrt m\,T^{3/2}$. Adding and dividing by $W$: $u(a,\bar y)-u(\bar x,\beta)\le\Delta(\sqrt n+\sqrt m)T^{3/2}\cdot 2/(T(T+1))\le 2\Delta(\sqrt n+\sqrt m)/\sqrt T$. Since $\max_a u(a,\bar y)\ge\operatorname{val}(M)$ (the maximin is at most the payoff of the best pure row against any fixed $\bar y$), the $\varepsilon_s$ bound follows. $\square$

**Corollary A′.** With Proposition 4 at every live $(t,s)$ ($n_t,m_t$ the stage menu sizes, $\Delta$ the stage range, $T$ iterations), Theorem A gives $\operatorname{expl}(\pi)\le\sum_{t<H}\sup_s\big[2\Delta(\sqrt{n_t}+\sqrt{m_t})/\sqrt T+2\varepsilon_v(t,s)\big]\le H\big(4\Delta\sqrt{\max(n,m)}/\sqrt T+2\varepsilon_v\big)$. In the targets' notation $c\,R\sqrt{\max(n,m)}/\sqrt T$ with $R=\Delta$: $c=4$ (linear averaging), $c=2$ (uniform).

**What the code does differently (nash.py:47–61).** `rm_plus` is *alternating*: at iteration $t$ the row accumulator adds the regret of the pair $(x_{t-1},y_{t-1})$ and produces $x_t$; the column accumulator then adds the regret of $y_{t-1}$ against the *updated* $x_t$ and produces $y_t$; both averages weight $x_1..x_T$, $y_1..y_T$ by $t$. Lemma 3 still bounds both accumulators (its proof only needs each strategy to be proportional to its accumulator *before* the next regret is added, which holds). But the two players' regrets are now measured on different utility sequences, and the folk conversion leaves a cross term. With uniform averaging of $\bar x=\frac1T\sum_{t=1}^Tx_t$ and $\bar y'=\frac1T\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}y_t$:
$$\max_a u(a,\bar y')-\min_\beta u(\bar x,\beta)\le\frac{R^x_T+R^y_T}{T}+\frac1T\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}\big[u(x_t,y_t)-u(x_{t+1},y_t)\big],\tag{4.1}$$
proved by subtracting $\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}u(a,y_t)\le\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}u(x_t,y_t)+R^x_T$ from $\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}u(x_{t+1},\beta)\ge\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}u(x_{t+1},y_t)-R^y_T$. The cross term is minus the row player's one-step improvement against $y_t$; it is $\le0$ term by term, because an RM+ update never lowers the updating player's utility against the vector that produced it (Burch, Moravčík & Schmid, JAIR 2019, *Revisiting CFR+ and alternating updates* — **not in lit review, check**, Thm 5; Lean `rmplus_improvement`). (The same paper shows the naive folk theorem can fail for non-RM alternating strategies.) The same argument covers the code's exact indexing. Write $r^x_{t+1}$ for the row regret of $(x_t,y_t)$ and add one virtual row update; then $W[u(a,\bar y)-u(\bar x,\beta)]=\sum_t t\,r^x_{t+1}(a)+\sum_t t\,r^y_t(\beta)+\sum_t t\,[u(x_t,y_t)-u(x_t,y_{t-1})]$, and the last sum is $\le0$ term by term because the column player's RM+ update does not decrease its own utility against $x_t$. Lemma 3's weighted bound then gives $\text{gap}\le 2\Delta(\sqrt{n(T+1)}+\sqrt{mT})/(T+1)\le 2\Delta(\sqrt n+\sqrt m)/\sqrt T$, so $\varepsilon_s\le\text{gap}\le 2\Delta(\sqrt n+\sqrt m)/\sqrt T$ for `rm_plus` as coded (cf. BMS19 Thm 8). Proposition 4's bound, and Corollary A′ with $c=4$, hold for the code's alternating scheme. **Use:** Proposition 4 fixes the worst-case *shape* $\varepsilon_s=O(\Delta\sqrt{\max(n,m)/T})$; the harness measures $\varepsilon_s$ directly (RESULTS.md, "Convergence rates": median Nash gap $6.3\cdot10^{-4}\Delta$ at $T=100$, fitted $\propto T^{-1.46}$, slope quartiles $-1.68/-1.46/-1.25$), far below the worst case, as is usual for RM+ with alternation and linear averaging; RM+ last-iterate theory is weak (Cai23, Far23), so only the averaged strategy carries a guarantee.

## 5. Theorem B (hidden information, one stage, $K$ sampled worlds)

**Setting.** Finite type set $\Theta$, belief $b\in\Delta(\Theta)$; for each $\theta$ a matrix $M_\theta\in\mathbb R^{A\times B_\theta}$ with all entries in an interval of length $\Delta$ (common across $\theta$). The opponent knows $\theta$; the agent plays one $x\in\Delta(A)$. Agent-form objective (L1 of `math_framing.md`; `agent_form_lp`):
$$g_\theta(x)=\min_{\beta\in B_\theta}(M_\theta^\top x)_\beta,\qquad f_b(x)=\sum_\theta b(\theta)g_\theta(x),\qquad v(b)=\max_xf_b(x).$$
The agent has an estimate $\hat b$, draws $\theta_1,\dots,\theta_K\stackrel{iid}\sim\hat b$, uses estimated matrices $\hat M_\theta$ with $\|\hat M_\theta-M_\theta\|_\infty\le\varepsilon_v$, and outputs $\hat x$ that is $\varepsilon_s$-optimal for $\hat f_K(x)=\frac1K\sum_k\hat g_{\theta_k}(x)$ (with $\hat g$ built from $\hat M$). Let $f_K(x)=\frac1K\sum_kg_{\theta_k}(x)$ (true matrices, sampled types) and $S=\sup_{x\in\Delta(A)}|f_{\hat b}(x)-f_K(x)|$.

**Lemma 5 (regularity of $g,f$).** (a) $|g_\theta(x)-g_\theta(x')|\le\frac\Delta2\|x-x'\|_1$. (b) $g_\theta$ and $f_b$ are concave in $x$. (c) $|f_b(x)-f_{\hat b}(x)|\le\Delta\,\mathrm{TV}(b,\hat b)$. (d) If $\|\hat M_\theta-M_\theta\|_\infty\le\varepsilon_v$ then $|\hat g_\theta(x)-g_\theta(x)|\le\varepsilon_v$.

*Proof.* Let $c$ be the midpoint of the entry interval, so $|M_\theta[a,\beta]-c|\le\Delta/2$. (a) $\min$ is 1-Lipschitz in sup norm, and $|\sum_a(x_a-x'_a)M_\theta[a,\beta]|=|\sum_a(x_a-x'_a)(M_\theta[a,\beta]-c)|\le\frac\Delta2\|x-x'\|_1$ because $\sum_a(x_a-x'_a)=0$. (b) $g_\theta$ is a minimum of linear functions; $f_b$ is a non-negative combination. (c) $g_\theta(x)\in[c-\Delta/2,c+\Delta/2]$ and $\sum_\theta(b-\hat b)(\theta)=0$, so $|\sum_\theta(b-\hat b)(\theta)(g_\theta(x)-c)|\le\frac\Delta2\|b-\hat b\|_1=\Delta\,\mathrm{TV}$. (d) Lemma 1(i) with $y=e_\beta$, then 1-Lipschitzness of $\min$. $\square$

**Theorem B (decomposition).** $v(b)-f_b(\hat x)\le\varepsilon_s+2\Delta\,\mathrm{TV}(b,\hat b)+2\varepsilon_v+2S.$

*Proof.* Chain of seven inequalities, each by Lemma 5 or by definition:
$v(b)\le v(\hat b)+\Delta\mathrm{TV}$ (5c at the maximiser of $f_b$) $\le\max_xf_K(x)+\Delta\mathrm{TV}+S$ $\le\max_x\hat f_K(x)+\Delta\mathrm{TV}+S+\varepsilon_v$ (5d) $\le\hat f_K(\hat x)+\varepsilon_s+\Delta\mathrm{TV}+S+\varepsilon_v$ $\le f_K(\hat x)+\varepsilon_s+\Delta\mathrm{TV}+S+2\varepsilon_v$ (5d) $\le f_{\hat b}(\hat x)+\varepsilon_s+\Delta\mathrm{TV}+2S+2\varepsilon_v$ $\le f_b(\hat x)+\varepsilon_s+2\Delta\mathrm{TV}+2S+2\varepsilon_v$ (5c). $\square$

*Remark 1 (tightness of $2\Delta\,\mathrm{TV}$).* Three types, two actions, one opponent column per type, $0<t\le\frac12$: $M_1=(\frac\Delta2,-\frac\Delta2)^\top$, $M_2=(-\frac\Delta2,\frac\Delta2)^\top$, $M_3=\frac{t\Delta}{2(1-t)}(1,-1)^\top$, $b=(t,0,1-t)$, $\hat b=(0,t,1-t)$, $\mathrm{TV}=t$. Then $f_{\hat b}\equiv 0$ on the simplex, so $\hat x=e_2$ is exactly optimal for $\hat b$, while $f_b(e_1)=t\Delta=v(b)$ and $f_b(e_2)=-t\Delta$: loss $2t\Delta=2\Delta\,\mathrm{TV}$. Lemma 5(c) is tight as well, so no better constant exists.

*Remark 2 (the targets' constant).* The targets state the belief term as $2R\,\mathrm{TV}$ with "payoffs in $[-R,R]$". With $\Delta=2R$ the correct term is $4R\,\mathrm{TV}$; the statement is correct only if $R$ is the range, which is the convention in `math_framing.md` (L2) and presumably in the 2,000-instance check (max ratio 0.48 — consistent with either reading, so the check does not settle it). For our $[-1,1]$ payoffs the term is $4\,\mathrm{TV}$. The $2\varepsilon_v$ and $2S$ factors in the targets are correct.

**Lemma 6 (covering the simplex).** For $0<\varepsilon\le 1$ there is a set $N_\varepsilon\subset\Delta(A)$, $|N_\varepsilon|\le(3/\varepsilon)^{|A|}$, with every $x\in\Delta(A)$ within $\ell_1$-distance $\varepsilon$ of $N_\varepsilon$.

*Proof.* Let $N_\varepsilon$ be a maximal $\varepsilon$-separated subset of $\Delta(A)$ (then it is an $\varepsilon$-net by maximality). The $\ell_1$ balls of radius $\varepsilon/2$ around its points are disjoint and lie in the $\ell_1$ ball of radius $1+\varepsilon/2$ (as $\Delta(A)\subset B_1(0,1)$). Comparing volumes in $\mathbb R^{|A|}$: $|N_\varepsilon|(\varepsilon/2)^{|A|}\le(1+\varepsilon/2)^{|A|}$, so $|N_\varepsilon|\le(1+2/\varepsilon)^{|A|}\le(3/\varepsilon)^{|A|}$. $\square$

**Theorem B′ (uniform sampling error).** Let $n=|A|$, $K\ge 9$, and $\theta_k\stackrel{iid}\sim\hat b$.
(i) For $\delta\in(0,1)$, with probability $\ge1-\delta$: $\;S\le\Delta\sqrt{2\,(n\log K+\log(2/\delta))/K}$.
(ii) $\mathbb E\,S\le\Delta\sqrt{2\,(n\log K+\log 2)/K}$.

*Proof.* $D(x)=f_K(x)-f_{\hat b}(x)=\frac1K\sum_k[g_{\theta_k}(x)-\mathbb Eg_\theta(x)]$ is an average of $K$ iid centred variables with range $\le\Delta$ (Lemma 5c's bound on $g$), and is $\Delta$-Lipschitz in $\ell_1$ (two $\frac\Delta2$-Lipschitz functions, Lemma 5a). Take $\varepsilon=K^{-1/2}\le 1$ and the net $N_\varepsilon$ of Lemma 6, $N:=|N_\varepsilon|\le(3\sqrt K)^n$; for $K\ge 9$, $3\sqrt K\le K$ so $\log N\le n\log K$. For every $x$ pick $x_i\in N_\varepsilon$ with $\|x-x_i\|_1\le\varepsilon$: $|D(x)|\le|D(x_i)|+\Delta\varepsilon$, so $S\le\max_i|D(x_i)|+\Delta/\sqrt K$.
(i) Hoeffding: $\Pr(|D(x_i)|\ge u)\le 2e^{-2Ku^2/\Delta^2}$. Union bound over $N$ points: with probability $\ge 1-\delta$, $\max_i|D(x_i)|\le\Delta\sqrt{\log(2N/\delta)/(2K)}\le\Delta\sqrt{(n\log K+\log(2/\delta))/(2K)}$. Hence $S\le\frac{\Delta}{\sqrt K}\big[\sqrt{(n\log K+\log(2/\delta))/2}+1\big]$. Since $n\log K\ge\log 9>2$, the bracket's second term is at most the first, giving the stated form.
(ii) By Hoeffding's lemma each $D(x_i)$ is sub-Gaussian with variance proxy $\Delta^2/(4K)$; the maximal inequality for the $2N$ variables $\pm D(x_i)$ gives $\mathbb E\max_i|D(x_i)|\le\frac{\Delta}{2\sqrt K}\sqrt{2\log(2N)}=\Delta\sqrt{\log(2N)/(2K)}$. Conclude as in (i). $\square$

*Remark (vacuity at small $K$).* Since $S\le\Delta$ trivially, (ii) is informative only when $K/\log K\gtrsim2|A|$ (e.g. $|A|=3$ needs $K\gtrsim 30$); at the engine's $K$ the measured $S$ should be used instead. Measured $\mathbb ES=0.054\Delta$ at $|A|=3$, $K=25$, against the bound $0.91\Delta$ (`checks/check_theorem_b.py`).

In the targets' form $C\,R\sqrt{(|A|\log K+\log(1/\delta))/K}$: with $R=\Delta$, $C=\sqrt2$ (and $\log(2/\delta)$ in place of $\log(1/\delta)$), valid for $K\ge 9$. Version (ii) is the one the full game needs (§6). In the engine $\hat b$ is the generator's joint prior restricted by hard evidence, so $\mathrm{TV}(b,\hat b)$ is behavioural-modelling error only (`math_framing.md` §3.3), and $\varepsilon_s\approx 0$ because `agent_form_lp` solves the $K$-world game exactly.

## 6. Theorem C (hidden information, full game)

### 6.1 What the literature provides (cited, not proved here)

Depth-limited solving with re-solving in imperfect-information games is exploitability-safe only if the leaf values are **functions of both players' reach probabilities (ranges) at a public state** and re-solving is constrained so the opponent cannot profit from the agent's revised beliefs:
- CFR-D (Burch, Johanson & Bowling, AAAI 2014, *Solving imperfect information games using decomposition* — **not in lit review, check**): the trunk is solved with a counterfactual-value vector per public state; subgames are re-solved through a gadget that gives the opponent the option of its trunk counterfactual values. Exploitability is bounded by the sum of trunk and subgame regret terms; re-solving with exact counterfactual values does not increase exploitability (the "safe" property, cf. Bro17 §4.2: "Resolving guarantees that P2's exploitability will be no higher than the blueprint's").
- DeepStack (Mor17), Theorem 1 of the supplement (wording from memory — check): if the counterfactual-value network has error $\le\epsilon$ and each re-solve runs $T$ iterations, exploitability $\le k_1\epsilon+k_2/\sqrt T$, $k_1,k_2$ game-specific, $k_1$ growing with the number of re-solves along a line of play.
- ReBeL (Bro20), Theorem 3: with no off-policy exploration at test time, a value network of error $\le\delta$ trained as in Theorem 2, and $T$ CFR iterations per subgame, the algorithm plays a $(\delta C_1+\delta C_2/\sqrt T)$-Nash equilibrium ($C_1,C_2$ game-specific). Depth-limited solving with multi-valued states (Bro18) and value functions over public states as functions of ranges (Kovařík, Seitz, Lisý, Rudolf, Sun & Ha, *Artificial Intelligence* 314:103805, 2023, arXiv 1906.06412 — cited in the lit review text as Kov19b but **no BibTeX entry; check**) make the same point: a single scalar per (state, world) is not a sufficient leaf.

Schematically: $\operatorname{expl}\le(\text{solver regret})+(\#\text{public stages})\times(\text{PBS value error})$, **provided** the leaf is a range-dependent value vector and re-solving is safe. Our agent satisfies neither proviso: its leaf $\hat V(s',\theta)$ is a per-world full-information value, and it re-solves each turn from a belief updated by hard evidence only. §6.3 quantifies both gaps.

### 6.2 Model (one-sided private information)

Public state $s\in S$ (including the agent's own team: the model concedes it to the opponent, as PokaiTrainer does, Yu26 §4.1), private type $\theta\in\Theta$ of the opponent drawn once from the prior $b_0$ and known only to the opponent, transitions $P_\theta(s'\mid s,a,\beta)$, menus $A(s)$, $B_\theta(s)$, terminal payoff $u(s,\theta)\in[-1,1]$ (allowed to depend on $\theta$; cap payoff $u_H(s,\theta)$). Public history $h_t=(s_0,a_0,\beta_0,\dots,s_t)$; the opponent's strategy $\sigma_\theta(h_t)\in\Delta(B_\theta(s_t))$ may depend on $\theta$ and the history. Under a given $\sigma$ the **true posterior** is $p_{h_0}=b_0$ and
$$p_{h_ta\beta s'}(\theta)\propto p_{h_t}(\theta)\,\sigma_\theta(h_t)(\beta)\,P_\theta(s'\mid s_t,a,\beta).\tag{6.1}$$
For $(t,s,b)$ let $\Gamma_t(s,b)$ be the game from stage $t$ at public state $s$ with $\theta\sim b$ told to the opponent, and $V_t(s,b)$ its value (exists: finite, perfect recall). Convention: $V_t(s,b)=\sum_\theta b(\theta)u(s,\theta)$ at terminal $s$, and $V_H(s,b)=\sum_\theta b(\theta)u_H(s,\theta)$. $V_t(s,\delta_\theta)=V^*_t(s,\theta)$ is the full-information value of world $\theta$.

**Lemma 7 (structure of $V_t(s,\cdot)$).** (a) $V_t(s,\cdot)$ is convex and $2$-Lipschitz in TV on $\Delta(\Theta)$ (2 = the terminal payoff range). (b) For live $s$, with $\Phi^{s,t}_b(x,y)=\sum_{a,\beta,s'}x_a\,n_b(a,\beta,s')\,V_{t+1}(s',b'_{a,\beta,s'})$ where $n_b(a,\beta,s')=\sum_\theta b(\theta)y_\theta(\beta)P_\theta(s'\mid s,a,\beta)$ and $b'(\theta)=b(\theta)y_\theta(\beta)P_\theta(s'\mid s,a,\beta)/n_b$ (terms with $n_b=0$ dropped):
$$V_t(s,b)\le\min_{y\in\prod_\theta\Delta(B_\theta(s))}\max_{x\in\Delta(A(s))}\Phi_b(x,y)=\max_x\min_y\Phi_b(x,y)=:\max_xG_b(x).$$

*Proof.* (a) For a fixed agent behavioural strategy $\pi$ the opponent's types face separate problems, so $\inf_\sigma\mathbb E_{b,\pi,\sigma}[u]=\sum_\theta b(\theta)\ell_\pi(\theta)$ with $\ell_\pi(\theta)=\inf_{\sigma_\theta}\mathbb E_{\theta,\pi,\sigma_\theta}[u]\in[-1,1]$. Hence $V_t(s,b)=\max_\pi\sum_\theta b(\theta)\ell_\pi(\theta)$ is a maximum of linear functions of $b$ (convex), each with coefficients in $[-1,1]$ (Lipschitz, as in Lemma 5c with range 2). (b) *Inequality:* fix $y$. Let the opponent play $y_\theta$ at $(t,s)$ and then, at each $(s',b')$, an optimal strategy of type $\theta$ in $\Gamma_{t+1}(s',b')$ (it exists; the opponent knows $y$ and hence $b'$). The true posterior after $(a,\beta,s')$ is exactly $b'$ by (6.1), whatever the agent's $x$ (the agent's action is public and $x$ is type-independent). Any agent continuation from $h'$ is a strategy of $\Gamma_{t+1}(s',b')$, so its payoff is $\le V_{t+1}(s',b')$. Thus any agent strategy gets at most $\Phi_b(x,y)\le\max_x\Phi_b(x,y)$, and $V_t(s,b)\le\min_y\max_x\Phi_b$. *Equality:* $\Phi_b$ is linear in $x$ and convex in $y$: each term is $x_a\cdot\operatorname{persp}_{V_{t+1}(s',\cdot)}(m(y))$ where $m(y)=(b(\theta)y_\theta(\beta)P_\theta(s'))_\theta$ is linear in $y$ and $\operatorname{persp}_V(m)=\|m\|_1V(m/\|m\|_1)$ (0 at 0) is convex on $\mathbb R^\Theta_{\ge0}$ for convex $V$ (for $m,m'$ with masses $\mu,\mu'$: $\operatorname{persp}(\lambda m+(1-\lambda)m')=(\lambda\mu+(1-\lambda)\mu')V\big(\frac{\lambda\mu}{\lambda\mu+(1-\lambda)\mu'}\frac m\mu+\dots\big)\le\lambda\mu V(\frac m\mu)+(1-\lambda)\mu'V(\frac{m'}{\mu'})$). $V_{t+1}(s',\cdot)$ is continuous by (a), so $\Phi_b$ is continuous; both domains are compact convex; Sion's theorem gives $\min_y\max_x=\max_x\min_y$. $\square$

(The reverse inequality in (b) — the full recursive formula — holds too but is not needed here.)

**Agent.** Markov in $(t,s,b^{\mathrm{hard}})$, where $b^{\mathrm{hard}}_{h_0}=b_0$ and
$$b^{\mathrm{hard}}_{h_ta\beta s'}(\theta)\propto b^{\mathrm{hard}}_{h_t}(\theta)\,\mathbf 1[\beta\in B_\theta(s_t)]\,P_\theta(s'\mid s_t,a,\beta)\tag{6.2}$$
(legality and chance likelihoods only; L4(i)). At a live $(t,s,b)$ the agent: draws $K$ worlds iid from $b$, fresh and independent of everything else and unobserved by the opponent; builds $\hat M_\theta$ with $\|\hat M_\theta-M_\theta\|_\infty\le\varepsilon_v(t,s)$ where $M_\theta(a,\beta)=\sum_{s'}P_\theta(s'\mid s,a,\beta)V_{t+1}(s',\delta_\theta)$ is the exact **revealed-world** matrix (`full_matrix` per world; by Lemma 2(c) $\varepsilon_v\le\varepsilon_{\mathrm{leaf}}$ for the per-world leaf); and plays $\hat x$ that is $\varepsilon_s(t,s,b)$-optimal in $\hat f_K$. Let $f_b(x)=\sum_\theta b(\theta)\min_\beta(M_\theta^\top x)_\beta$ (the agent-form objective with exact revealed-world matrices) and $\tilde x_t(s,b)=\mathbb E_{\text{samples}}[\hat x]$.

**Definition (non-locality bias).** For live $(t,s)$,
$$\eta(t,s',b')=\sum_\theta b'(\theta)V_{t+1}(s',\delta_\theta)-V_{t+1}(s',b')\;\ge0,\qquad\eta_{\mathrm{NL}}(t)=\sup_{s',\,b'\in\Delta(\Theta)}\eta(t,s',b').$$
Non-negativity is Jensen with Lemma 7(a). $\eta$ is the value, to the agent, of having $\theta$ revealed at $(s',b')$. It is exactly the gap between our leaf and a public-belief-state leaf: the PBS leaf is the vector $(V_{t+1}(s',b')\text{ and its per-type counterfactual values})$ as a function of the belief $b'$ that the stage strategies induce; ours is the fully-revealed point $V_{t+1}(s',\delta_\theta)$ of that object, independent of $b'$. Using it (i) lets the agent's continuation differ per world (strategy fusion, Lon10/Fra98) and (ii) decouples the types in the stage game although the true continuation $V_{t+1}(s',b')$ couples them through $b'$ (non-locality: $b'$ depends on what *other* types do at this stage). $\eta_{\mathrm{NL}}$ does not depend on $T$, $K$ or $\varepsilon_v$.

### 6.3 The theorem

**Theorem C (proved here).** Under the model of §6.2 with exact prior $b_0$, for every history-dependent type-informed $\sigma$, writing $\delta_C(t,h)=\varepsilon_s(t,s_t,b^{\mathrm{hard}}_h)+2\varepsilon_v(t,s_t)+2\,\mathbb E[S_t(s_t,b^{\mathrm{hard}}_h)]+\eta_{\mathrm{NL}}(t)+2\Delta\,\mathrm{TV}(p_h,b^{\mathrm{hard}}_h)$ on live stages (0 otherwise),
$$\mathbb E_{\pi,\sigma}[u]\ge V_0(s_0,b_0)-\mathbb E_{\pi,\sigma}\Big[\sum_{t<\tau}\delta_C(t,h_t)\Big],\qquad\text{hence}\qquad\operatorname{expl}(\pi)\le\sup_\sigma\mathbb E_{\pi,\sigma}\Big[\sum_{t<\tau}\delta_C(t,h_t)\Big]\le\sum_{t<H}\sup_{h}\delta_C(t,h).\tag{C.1}$$
Here $S_t(s,b)$ is the sampling deviation of Theorem B at the agent's $K$ draws from $b$, so by Theorem B′(ii) $2\mathbb ES_t\le2\Delta\sqrt{2(|A|\log K+\log 2)/K}$ for $K\ge9$; $\tau$ is the length; $\operatorname{expl}(\pi)=V_0(s_0,b_0)-\inf_\sigma\mathbb E_{\pi,\sigma}[u]$.

*Proof.* Because the samples are fresh, independent and unobserved, the opponent faces the behavioural strategy with stage mixtures $\tilde x_t(s,b)$; exploitability is a functional of that strategy, so we analyse $\tilde x$. **Stage guarantee.** Fix a live $(t,s,b)$. For each sample draw, Theorem B with $\hat b=b$ (no belief error inside the solve) gives $f_b(\hat x)\ge v(b)-\varepsilon_s-2\varepsilon_v-2S_t$; by concavity of $f_b$ (Lemma 5b), $f_b(\tilde x)\ge\mathbb Ef_b(\hat x)\ge v(b)-e$, $e:=\varepsilon_s+2\varepsilon_v+2\mathbb ES_t$. For any $p\in\Delta(\Theta)$, Lemma 5(c) twice: $f_p(\tilde x)\ge f_b(\tilde x)-\Delta\mathrm{TV}(p,b)\ge v(b)-e-\Delta\mathrm{TV}\ge v(p)-e-2\Delta\mathrm{TV}(p,b)$. **Revealed vs true stage game.** For any stage profile $y$ and the $\Phi_p,G_p$ of Lemma 7(b): the revealed payoff $\mathrm{Rev}_p(x,y)=\sum_\theta p(\theta)\sum_{a,\beta,s'}x_ay_\theta(\beta)P_\theta(s')V_{t+1}(s',\delta_\theta)$ satisfies, regrouping by $(a,\beta,s')$ and using the definition of $\eta$,
$$\Phi_p(x,y)=\sum_{a,\beta,s'}x_an_p(a,\beta,s')V_{t+1}(s',b')\quad\in\big[\mathrm{Rev}_p(x,y)-\eta_{\mathrm{NL}}(t),\;\mathrm{Rev}_p(x,y)\big],$$
since $\sum_{a,\beta,s'}x_an_p=1$ and $0\le\eta\le\eta_{\mathrm{NL}}$. The types decouple in $\min_y\mathrm{Rev}_p(x,y)=f_p(x)$, so $f_p(x)-\eta_{\mathrm{NL}}(t)\le G_p(x)\le f_p(x)$ for all $x$. Therefore $G_p(\tilde x)\ge f_p(\tilde x)-\eta_{\mathrm{NL}}\ge\max_xf_p(x)-e-2\Delta\mathrm{TV}-\eta_{\mathrm{NL}}\ge\max_xG_p(x)-e-2\Delta\mathrm{TV}-\eta_{\mathrm{NL}}\ge V_t(s,p)-e-2\Delta\mathrm{TV}(p,b)-\eta_{\mathrm{NL}}(t)$, the last step by Lemma 7(b). **Induction.** Fix $\sigma$; let $W_t(h)$ be the expected payoff from $h$ under $(\tilde x,\sigma)$ with types distributed as $p_h$ (the recursion (1.1) with $\sum_\theta p_h(\theta)\sigma_\theta(h)(\beta)P_\theta$ in place of $\sigma(\beta)P$), and $D_t(h)=\mathbb E[\sum_{t\le t'<\tau}\delta_C(t',h_{t'})\mid h]$. Claim: $W_t(h)\ge V_t(s_t,p_h)-D_t(h)$. Base ($t=H$ or terminal): $W_t=\sum_\theta p_h(\theta)u(s_t,\theta)=V_t(s_t,p_h)$, $D_t=0$. Step: with $x=\tilde x_t(s_t,b^{\mathrm{hard}}_h)$, $y=\sigma(h)$, and the posterior $p_{h'}$ of (6.1) being exactly the $b'$ of Lemma 7(b) for $(p_h,y)$:
$$W_t(h)\ge\sum_{a,\beta,s'}x_an_{p_h}(a,\beta,s')\big[V_{t+1}(s',p_{h'})-D_{t+1}(h')\big]=\Phi_{p_h}(x,y)-\big(D_t(h)-\delta_C(t,h)\big)\ge G_{p_h}(x)-D_t(h)+\delta_C(t,h),$$
and by the stage guarantee $G_{p_h}(x)\ge V_t(s_t,p_h)-\delta_C(t,h)$. At the root $p_{h_0}=b_0$, giving the first claim; take $\inf_\sigma$ for (C.1). $\square$

**Corollary C.1 (the targets' form, with its hypothesis made explicit).** If the opponent is **non-revealing** — $\sigma_\theta(h)(\beta)$ does not depend on $\theta$ among the types with $\beta\in B_\theta(s_t)$ — then $p_h=b^{\mathrm{hard}}_h$ for all $h$ (induction on (6.1)–(6.2)), the TV term vanishes, and with Proposition 4 and Theorem B′(ii):
$$\operatorname{expl}(\pi)\le\sum_{t<H}\Big[\frac{2\Delta(\sqrt{n_t}+\sqrt{m_t})}{\sqrt T}+2\Delta\sqrt{\tfrac{2(|A|\log K+\log2)}{K}}+2\varepsilon_v+\eta_{\mathrm{NL}}(t)\Big]\le H\Big(a\,T^{-1/2}+b\sqrt{\tfrac{|A|\log K}{K}}+2\varepsilon_v+\eta_{\mathrm{NL}}\Big)$$
with $a=4\Delta\sqrt{\max(n,m)}$, $b=2\Delta\sqrt{2(1+\log 2/\log K)}\le 4\Delta$ ($K\ge 9$), $\eta_{\mathrm{NL}}=\max_t\eta_{\mathrm{NL}}(t)$. (If `agent_form_lp` is used, $\varepsilon_s=0$ and the $T$ term disappears; the stage opponent's "action" in the agent-form game is a profile, so with RM+ per world the $\sqrt{m_t}$ is $\max_\theta\sqrt{|B_\theta|}$.)

**Against arbitrary opponents the TV term is necessary: the targets' four-term form is false.** *Example C.1 (three types).* $\Theta=\{1,2,3\}$, $b_0$ uniform. Stage 0: the agent has one action; the opponent picks $\beta\in\{1,2,3\}$, moving to public $s_\beta$; no chance. Stage 1 (last): at $s_\beta$ the agent picks risky or safe. Risky pays $-1$ against type $\beta$ and $+c$ against the other two types; safe pays $0$; $c=\frac34$ (any $c\in(\frac12,1]$ works). The agent solves $f_{b^{\mathrm{hard}}}$ exactly ($K\to\infty$, or `agent_form_lp` on the exact belief), so the sampling term is zero. The hard-evidence belief at every $s_\beta$ is uniform, and risky has margin $(2c-1)/3>0$ against it, so the agent plays risky everywhere (uniquely; $\varepsilon_s=0$), and $\varepsilon_v=0$ (terminal payoffs). Each type routes to its own state $s_\beta$, so the agent gets $-1$. The value is $V_0=0$ (play safe), so $\operatorname{expl}=1$. The non-locality terms are $\eta_{\mathrm{NL}}(1)=0$ ($V_2$ is linear in $b$) and $\eta_{\mathrm{NL}}(0)=c/(1+c)=\frac37$, so $\sum_t\eta_{\mathrm{NL}}(t)=\frac37$ and $H\max_t\eta_{\mathrm{NL}}=\frac67$, both below $\operatorname{expl}=1$: the four-term form (in either its sum or $H\max$ form) is violated. The five-term bound (C.1) is satisfied: at stage 0 the TV term is $0$ and $\eta_{\mathrm{NL}}(0)=\frac37$; at stage 1 the TV term is $2\Delta\cdot\frac23$ (true posterior $\delta_\beta$ vs. uniform $b^{\mathrm{hard}}$; $\Delta=\frac74$ gives $\frac73$); the total is $\ge1=\operatorname{expl}$. (Check scripts: `checks/example_c1_repaired.py`, `checks/search_3types.py`.)

*Remark (why three types).* In two-stage games of this shape, $\operatorname{expl}\le|\Theta|\,\eta_{\mathrm{NL}}(0)$: with $\ell_{\beta\theta}=V_1(s_\beta,\delta_\theta)-g_{\beta\theta}(x_\beta)\ge0$, $\sum_\theta b_0(\theta)\ell_{\beta\theta}=\eta(0,s_\beta,b_0)$, so $\operatorname{expl}\le\sum_\theta b_0(\theta)\max_\beta\ell_{\beta\theta}\le|\Theta|\,\eta_{\mathrm{NL}}(0)$. Hence at least 3 types are needed to break the $H\max$ form at $H=2$.

*Two-type illustration (not a counterexample to the four-term form).* $\Theta=\{1,2\}$, $b_0=(\frac12,\frac12)$, $\Delta=2$; stage 1 payoffs $u_{s_L}(a,\theta)$: $(a_1,1)\mapsto1,(a_2,1)\mapsto0,(a_1,2)\mapsto-1,(a_2,2)\mapsto\frac12$; $u_{s_R}$ the same with the types swapped. Then $V_0=\operatorname{expl}=\frac15$ (opponent commits at $q=(\frac35,\frac25)$) and the hard-evidence agent plays $a_2$ uniquely, but $\eta_{\mathrm{NL}}(1)=0$ while $\eta_{\mathrm{NL}}(0)=\frac35$ (at $s_L$, $b'=(\frac35,\frac25)$), so the four-term form (giving $\ge\frac35$) is consistent with $\operatorname{expl}=\frac15$.

So: **the $H$-fold additive decomposition does hold in the imperfect-information case**, but with five per-stage terms, and the fifth — the gap between the agent's belief and the true posterior under the opponent's actual behaviour — is controlled by the adversary (up to $2\Delta$ per stage) unless the agent models behaviour correctly or re-solves safely. This is precisely what CFR-D's gadget / DeepStack's constraints buy: they let the solve itself choose the opponent's reach probabilities adversarially, which replaces the TV term by solver and value-error terms (§6.1, cited). Without that, the honest statement for our agent is (C.1); the targets' four-term form is Corollary C.1 and holds only against non-revealing opponents (which an RL best response is not).

**Remark (two-sided information).** The engine also hides the agent's team. Let $\Gamma^{\mathrm{real}}$ be that game and $\Gamma^{\mathrm{conc}}$ the §6.2 model (team conceded). An opponent who knows more does at least as well, so $\inf_\sigma\mathbb E^{\mathrm{real}}_{\pi,\sigma}[u]\ge\inf_\sigma\mathbb E^{\mathrm{conc}}_{\pi,\sigma}[u]$, hence $\operatorname{expl}^{\mathrm{real}}(\pi)\le\operatorname{expl}^{\mathrm{conc}}(\pi)+\big(V^{\mathrm{real}}_0-V^{\mathrm{conc}}_0\big)$: the value of concealing our own team (L5) is added once, not per stage, and is not reduced by $T,K,\varepsilon_v$.

**Status of the parts.** Proved here: Lemmas 1–7, Theorems A, B, B′, C, Proposition 4, Corollary C.1, Example C.1, the two-sided remark. Cited: the four black boxes listed at the top; the DeepStack/ReBeL/CFR-D/Kovařík bounds of §6.1 (not used in any proof). Conjecture: $\sum_t\eta_{\mathrm{NL}}(t)$ along typical play is of the order of the measured oracle gap ($\approx0.1$ score, RESULTS.md C5), which measures the game-level value of revealing $\theta$ to the agent — the same object as $\eta$ but integrated over the oracle's play rather than taken as a sup.

## 7. Fit form for the Modal experiments

The theory (C.1), with $\varepsilon_s$ from RM+ and $\mathbb ES$ from Theorem B′(ii), predicts an **upper bound** of the form
$$\operatorname{expl}(T,K,\mathrm{leaf})\;\lesssim\;H_{\mathrm{eff}}\big(a\,T^{-\alpha}+b\,K^{-1/2}\sqrt{\log K}+c\,\varepsilon_v\big)+d,$$
with worst-case $\alpha=\frac12$ (measured stage gaps give $1.46$), $c=2$, $b\le 4\Delta$, $a\le4\Delta\sqrt{\max(n,m)}$ (or $a=0$ with the exact LP), and $d=\sum_t\sup[\eta_{\mathrm{NL}}(t)+2\Delta\mathrm{TV}_t]+(V^{\mathrm{real}}_0-V^{\mathrm{conc}}_0)$, which does **not** vanish as $T,K\to\infty$, $\varepsilon_v\to0$. The targets' $d$ should be read as absorbing these three biases; it scales with length too, so a fit with a free $d$ cannot separate them from $H_{\mathrm{eff}}$.

Measurable in the harness: $T$, $K$, the leaf variant (categorical proxy for $\varepsilon_v$; $\varepsilon_v$ itself needs $V^*$ and is not measurable), $\varepsilon_s$ (Nash gap of the output in the solved stage game; already logged), $S$ (compare $f_K$ with a large-$K$ reference on logged beliefs, or the spread of $f_K$ across re-draws), $H_{\mathrm{eff}}$ (mean length of the games against the exploiter — by A2 the right length is the one under the best response, which the RL exploiter approximates), and the exploitability proxy itself. Two caveats for the fit: (i) an RL/local best response gives a **lower** bound on $\operatorname{expl}$ while the theory gives an upper bound, so the fit tests the functional form ($T^{-\alpha}$, $K^{-1/2}$, linear in leaf error, linear in $H_{\mathrm{eff}}$), not the constants; (ii) the $T$ and $K$ axes should saturate at the floor $d$, and the floor's dependence on the exploiter's adaptivity is the behavioural-TV term (compare a type-independent scripted opponent, where Corollary C.1 applies, with the RL exploiter).
